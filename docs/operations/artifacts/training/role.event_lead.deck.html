Event Lead

Training deck

Per-match operational lead. Owns the run-of-show for an individual match or event slot within a venue's event-day.

Why this role matters

You own the event experience. Check-ins, briefings, and the moments in between — you make game day feel organized to every coach, ref, and family who walks in.

What we believe

These four beliefs shape every practice plan, every sideline conversation, and every call we make about a kid.

Development Over Winning

We measure success by effort, improvement, and enjoyment — not the scoreboard. Winning takes care of itself when kids develop the right way.

Every Child Can Improve

There's no such thing as a "non-athletic" kid. With the right environment and encouragement, every child can grow their abilities — talent is built, not discovered.

What we believe (continued)

Long-Term Athlete Development

We're building athletes for age 25, not just age 8. We never trade away a child's long-term growth for a short-term result.

Holistic Growth

Sport develops the whole child — skills, game understanding, fitness, and confidence together. We pay attention to all of it, not just the physical side.

How that shows up day to day

We coach to two goals at once: compete to win, and grow every player's character and competence. When those two pull in different directions, character comes first.

Day to day, that means we praise effort, learning, and bouncing back from mistakes — the things a kid actually controls — over results they can't always control.

Whatever your role, this is what families should feel from us.

Referee assignment confirmation

You own this

When: 48 hours before kickoff (after assignor publishes schedule)

There's a checklist for this — see the checklist slides.

If something goes wrong: If Event Lead unreachable, escalate to Venue Manager per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. Once the assignor publishes the match schedule (around 48 hours
  2. before kickoff), pull the full match list for today's event window
  3. and check which matches have a referee crew assigned.
  4. For each assigned crew, check whether they've acknowledged the
  5. assignment through the assignor tool or contractor channel.
  6. Follow up directly with any ref who hasn't acknowledged — a quick
  7. message asking for a yes/no is enough at this stage.
  8. For any match still unassigned or any ref who declines, pull a
  9. replacement from the standby ref pool and confirm they can cover
  10. the match time and location.
  11. Watch for a ref double-booked across two matches that overlap in
  12. time, and reassign one of them before the day arrives.
  13. Confirm the crew size matches what the match format needs (center
  14. ref only vs. center plus assistants) for tournament or higher-level
  15. matches.
  16. Once every match on today's schedule shows a confirmed, acknowledged
  17. ref crew, mark the checklist complete.
  18. If a match still has no confirmed ref by T-24h, escalate per the
  19. escalation path — don't let it ride into game day unresolved.

Pre-shift staff briefing

You're part of this

When: About 60 minutes before first kickoff

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, Event Lead delivers the briefing and escalates to Director per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. About 60 minutes before first kickoff, once the facility unlock and
  2. first-aid kit check are done, gather every on-shift staff member in
  3. one spot — event leads, facilities, front of house, photographer.
  4. Keep it to 5-10 minutes. Cover today's schedule (match count, key
  5. times), anything special about today (VIP guests, a rescheduled
  6. match, a tournament format quirk), the weather outlook if outdoor,
  7. and any safety reminders (first-aid kit locations, incident
  8. escalation path).
  9. Call out any staffing changes from the schedule confirm — who's
  10. covering for a standby swap, who's new to the venue today.
  11. Ask if anyone has a conflict or needs to leave early, and confirm
  12. coverage for that window now rather than discovering it mid-shift.
  13. Pass around the sign-in sheet — every attendee signs to confirm
  14. they were briefed.
  15. Front of house checks the sign-in sheet against today's staffing
  16. plan and signs to confirm the full roster attended and the sheet is
  17. complete.
  18. File the signed sheet with today's event records.
  19. If the venue manager can't deliver the briefing, the event lead
  20. runs it and escalates per the escalation path.

Coach pregame briefing (youth league)

You own this

When: About 15 minutes before each youth-league kickoff

If something goes wrong: If Event Lead unreachable, escalate to Venue Manager per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. This runs for youth-league matches only, alongside team check-in,
  2. about 15 minutes before kickoff — adult leagues are self-managed
  3. and skip it entirely.
  4. Gather both teams' coaches together, not one at a time, so both
  5. hear the same information.
  6. Cover rules of the day: any format quirks (small-sided rules,
  7. quarter/half length for the age group), and anything from the
  8. coach pregame dispatch packet worth repeating out loud.
  9. Cover special situations: weather watch status if outdoor, any
  10. field/court condition notes, rescheduled or shortened matches.
  11. Restate conduct expectations — sideline coaching by parents,
  12. unsporting conduct, and that the code of conduct enforcement
  13. activity is what handles violations, not an on-field argument.
  14. Introduce the ref crew for the match if the coaches haven't already
  15. met them at this venue.
  16. Ask if either coach has a conflict to flag before kickoff (a
  17. player who needs to leave early, an injury from warmups).
  18. Each coach signs to acknowledge the briefing.
  19. If the event lead can't run the briefing, escalate to the venue
  20. manager per the escalation path rather than skipping it.

Photographer check-in

You're part of this

When: About 30 minutes before first assigned match

If something goes wrong: If Photographer no-show, Event Lead escalates to Venue Manager; pull from standby pool or accept reduced media coverage.

  1. About 30 minutes before your first assigned match, go to the event
  2. lead's station to sign in — this starts your pay clock for the
  3. session, so don't skip it even if you're already shooting
  4. warmups.
  5. Confirm your assignment against the event lead's schedule — venue,
  6. match(es), and session type (game, team posed, practice, event) —
  7. and flag any mismatch before signing.
  8. Confirm your gear: camera body, charged batteries plus a backup,
  9. memory cards with enough free space for the day, and any lens or
  10. flash the shot list calls for.
  11. Review the shot list or session notes with the event lead —
  12. required shots (team photos, action shots, specific players if
  13. requested), any restricted subjects, and the day's intended
  14. media-auth scope if it's already set (internal, promotional, or
  15. public), so you know what you're shooting for.
  16. Sign the check-in. The event lead countersigns to confirm your
  17. arrival and gear are in order.
  18. Once signed, your session moves from confirmed to checked-in in the
  19. platform — this is what the event lead and venue manager see
  20. reflected on the day's operations view.
  21. If you're a no-show, or running late enough that you'll miss the
  22. window, the event lead escalates to the venue manager immediately
  23. per the escalation path — don't wait until your scheduled start to
  24. raise it.

You own this

When: About 30 minutes before each kickoff

If something goes wrong: If Event Lead unreachable, escalate to Venue Manager per the standard handoff ladder; backup Referee pulled from standby pool if scheduled Referee no-shows.

  1. About 30 minutes before their assigned kickoff, the ref reports to
  2. the event lead's check-in station — the same match shows up in the
  3. ref's My Matches list on their phone.
  4. Confirm the ref's identity against today's confirmed assignment
  5. from the ref assignment confirm.
  6. Check the ref is in proper uniform (kit, badge/credential if
  7. required) and has their equipment — whistle, cards, watch.
  8. Confirm the ref knows their match: teams, field/court, kickoff
  9. time, and any special notes from the pregame weather or facility
  10. check.
  11. The ref signs in on the spot. This is what starts the stipend
  12. clock, so don't let a ref skip it and go straight to the field.
  13. Note the actual check-in time — if it's inside the 30-minute
  14. window but tight, give the ref a heads-up on time remaining before
  15. kickoff.
  16. If the scheduled ref doesn't show by check-in time, pull the backup
  17. from the standby pool immediately and check them in instead —
  18. don't wait past the window hoping the original ref appears.
  19. If the event lead is unavailable to run check-in, escalate to the
  20. venue manager per the escalation path.

You own this

When: About 15 minutes before each kickoff

If something goes wrong: If Event Lead unreachable, escalate to Venue Manager per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. About 15 minutes before kickoff, have both teams' coaches (youth
  2. leagues) or captains (adult leagues) come to the check-in station —
  3. this is the same check-in station families see when they arrive.
  4. Pull the team's roster on the check-in panel and confirm which
  5. players are physically present for today's match.
  6. Spot-check jerseys/uniforms match team colors so there's no
  7. confusion once play starts.
  8. If a guest player is being used (roster-eligible fill-in for a
  9. short-handed team), confirm their guest-player paperwork is on
  10. file before counting them as checked in.
  11. Flag any player who isn't on the roster at all — they can't play
  12. under this team's check-in; direct them to registration if they
  13. believe it's an error.
  14. Confirm both teams have enough checked-in players to meet the
  15. format's minimum to start (per the sport's roster rules).
  16. Submit the check-in form once both teams are confirmed — this both
  17. gates kickoff and records today's attendance.
  18. If either team can't field a legal minimum by kickoff, notify the
  19. ref and follow the format's forfeit/delay rule rather than
  20. starting short.

Code of conduct enforcement

You own this

When: Conduct issue observed or reported during the match

If something goes wrong: If Event Lead unreachable, escalate to Venue Manager; for any threatened violence escalate immediately to Director and law enforcement as appropriate.

  1. When a conduct issue is observed or reported during a match — a
  2. coach yelling from the sideline, a parent shouting at players or
  3. officials, someone ignoring the sideline boundary — respond in
  4. person and calmly. The goal is to de-escalate and get the match
  5. back to normal, not to make a scene.
  6. Start with the lightest effective step: a direct, private word with
  7. the person restating the expectation, not shouted across the
  8. field. Most sideline issues resolve here, especially for someone
  9. who just got heated in the moment.
  10. If the behavior continues after that first word, or it's serious
  11. enough to skip straight past a warning (targeting a player, abusive
  12. language at an official), issue a clear, specific warning: name the
  13. behavior, state what happens if it continues, and note who
  14. witnessed it.
  15. If the person doesn't comply with a warning, or the issue warrants
  16. it immediately, remove them — a parent goes to the parking lot or
  17. away from the team area; a coach who won't stop steps away from
  18. their team for the rest of the match (the assistant coach or team
  19. captain takes over).
  20. This is enforcement, not a referee ejection — you're not sending
  21. anyone from play under the sport's rules, and this isn't logged as
  22. an ejection. If what you're looking at is serious enough to be a
  23. formal ejection of a rostered player or coach, that's the ref's
  24. call, not yours.
  25. Log the incident in the form regardless of how it resolved: who was
  26. involved, what happened, what action you took, and who witnessed
  27. it. Even a fully-resolved verbal reminder gets logged — this is
  28. what lets the office see a pattern if the same person shows up
  29. again.
  30. Submit the form within about 5 minutes of the issue, while the
  31. details are fresh, and let the coach know afterward if a parent
  32. from their team was involved. Keep it factual and non-punitive in
  33. tone — the coach still owns that family relationship day to day.
  34. For anything involving a threat of violence or a safety concern,
  35. skip straight to removal and escalate immediately to the venue
  36. manager and, for a genuine safety threat, the director and law
  37. enforcement as appropriate. Don't try to talk someone down who's
  38. already crossed into a safety issue.

Incident response (in-the-moment)

You're part of this

When: Incident observed or reported during the match

If something goes wrong: Any life-threatening incident escalates immediately to 911 and Director; Venue Manager remains accountable for documentation but Event Lead may capture the initial form.

  1. The moment an incident is reported to you — injury, altercation,
  2. medical event, or property damage — get to the scene. If you
  3. didn't witness it yourself, find whoever did (coach, ref, event
  4. lead) before writing anything down.
  5. Scene safety and care come before documentation. If it's
  6. life-threatening, call 911 immediately — this also escalates
  7. directly to the director; don't wait to finish a form first.
  8. Never leave an injured or distressed child unattended while you
  9. sort out logistics.
  10. Get first-hand information from whoever responded: who was
  11. involved, what immediate care was given, whether 911 was called
  12. and when, and the exact time.
  13. Open the incident response form and capture what you have within
  14. about 5 minutes of the incident — this is the in-the-moment
  15. record, not the full report. It doesn't need to be complete, it
  16. needs to be fast and accurate: what happened, who responded, what
  17. care was given, whether 911 was called.
  18. If a parent is on-site, notify them directly and in person before
  19. they hear about it secondhand. Stick to what happened and what was
  20. done — don't speculate about diagnosis or fault.
  21. Handle this discreetly — this record names a minor. Don't discuss
  22. specifics in a group chat, in front of other families, or with
  23. anyone who doesn't need to know; the incident form is the record,
  24. not a text thread.
  25. Submit the form as soon as the immediate situation is stable. This
  26. starts the clock on the incident report finalization, which
  27. happens once the affected match is over.
  28. If you can't capture this yourself, the event lead may capture
  29. the initial form from the same first-hand accounts — you remain
  30. accountable for it either way.
  31. This form is the fast first pass, not the final record used for
  32. disputes or insurance — every incident captured here still needs
  33. the full finalized report before it's closed.

Ref stipend log

You own this

When: After Referee's last match of the day

If something goes wrong: If Event Lead unreachable, Venue Manager logs the stipend and escalates to Director for sign-off.

  1. After a ref's last match of the day, pull their check-in
  2. record(s) — one per match — to confirm which matches they
  3. actually worked today.
  4. Apply the standard per-match stipend rate for each match worked,
  5. then add any on-file add-ons (travel, doubleheader bonus,
  6. last-minute fill-in premium — whatever your org's pay schedule
  7. defines) on top of the base.
  8. Total the day's stipend across every match the ref worked and
  9. record the breakdown (base rate x number of matches, plus each
  10. add-on line) rather than just the final total — the ref and the
  11. office both need to see how the number was built.
  12. Walk the ref through the total before they leave and get their
  13. acknowledgment that it matches what they expect for today's work —
  14. resolve any discrepancy on the spot rather than after they've
  15. left.
  16. Record the stipend log — matches worked, base rate, add-ons,
  17. total, and the ref's acknowledgment — on the ref stipend log form.
  18. Submit the form. This hands the day's stipend record to the
  19. platform's payroll integration for the ref payroll event, which
  20. processes it for payout — you're not disbursing money yourself
  21. here, just logging what's owed and getting it acknowledged.
  22. If a ref disputes the total and it isn't resolved on the spot, log
  23. the dispute and the ref's stated expectation on the form rather
  24. than logging a number they haven't agreed to, and flag it to the
  25. venue manager.
  26. If you're unreachable, the venue manager logs the stipend and
  27. escalates to the director for sign-off.

Staff debrief

You're part of this

When: After facility close walkthrough is signed off

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, Event Lead runs the debrief and escalates to Director per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. Once the facility close walkthrough is signed off, gather whoever's
  2. still on shift for a 5-10 minute huddle — don't hold people who've
  3. already clocked out waiting for this.
  4. Ask what worked today: anything that ran smoother than expected,
  5. any process worth repeating.
  6. Ask what broke or ran rough: equipment failures, staffing gaps,
  7. confused parents, anything that slowed the day down.
  8. Ask if anything needs to go to the office: a facility repair
  9. request, a supply reorder, a schedule conflict for next time, a
  10. personnel note.
  11. Capture incident or safety mentions even briefly — the full
  12. incident report is a separate activity, but the debrief should
  13. flag that one happened so it isn't lost.
  14. Write the notes into the debrief form in plain language — this
  15. feeds directly into tonight's daily digest and the weekly review,
  16. so vague notes ("things were fine") aren't useful to future you.
  17. Submit the form before leaving the venue.
  18. If the venue manager isn't available, the event lead runs the
  19. debrief and escalates per the escalation path.
