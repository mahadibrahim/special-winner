Photographer

Training deck

Field-side contractor responsible for media capture per assignment, feeding the media tagging and publishing pipeline.

Why this role matters

You capture the proof. The memories families keep — and you guard the trust behind every photo of someone's child.

What we believe

These four beliefs shape every practice plan, every sideline conversation, and every call we make about a kid.

Development Over Winning

We measure success by effort, improvement, and enjoyment — not the scoreboard. Winning takes care of itself when kids develop the right way.

Every Child Can Improve

There's no such thing as a "non-athletic" kid. With the right environment and encouragement, every child can grow their abilities — talent is built, not discovered.

What we believe (continued)

Long-Term Athlete Development

We're building athletes for age 25, not just age 8. We never trade away a child's long-term growth for a short-term result.

Holistic Growth

Sport develops the whole child — skills, game understanding, fitness, and confidence together. We pay attention to all of it, not just the physical side.

How that shows up day to day

We coach to two goals at once: compete to win, and grow every player's character and competence. When those two pull in different directions, character comes first.

Day to day, that means we praise effort, learning, and bouncing back from mistakes — the things a kid actually controls — over results they can't always control.

Whatever your role, this is what families should feel from us.

Photographer check-in

You own this

When: About 30 minutes before first assigned match

If something goes wrong: If Photographer no-show, Event Lead escalates to Venue Manager; pull from standby pool or accept reduced media coverage.

  1. About 30 minutes before your first assigned match, go to the event
  2. lead's station to sign in — this starts your pay clock for the
  3. session, so don't skip it even if you're already shooting
  4. warmups.
  5. Confirm your assignment against the event lead's schedule — venue,
  6. match(es), and session type (game, team posed, practice, event) —
  7. and flag any mismatch before signing.
  8. Confirm your gear: camera body, charged batteries plus a backup,
  9. memory cards with enough free space for the day, and any lens or
  10. flash the shot list calls for.
  11. Review the shot list or session notes with the event lead —
  12. required shots (team photos, action shots, specific players if
  13. requested), any restricted subjects, and the day's intended
  14. media-auth scope if it's already set (internal, promotional, or
  15. public), so you know what you're shooting for.
  16. Sign the check-in. The event lead countersigns to confirm your
  17. arrival and gear are in order.
  18. Once signed, your session moves from confirmed to checked-in in the
  19. platform — this is what the event lead and venue manager see
  20. reflected on the day's operations view.
  21. If you're a no-show, or running late enough that you'll miss the
  22. window, the event lead escalates to the venue manager immediately
  23. per the escalation path — don't wait until your scheduled start to
  24. raise it.

Photo handoff (raw upload)

You own this

When: After Photographer's last match of the day

If something goes wrong: If upload fails, Photographer notifies Event Lead who escalates to Director; raw cards are retained until upload succeeds.

  1. After your last assigned match of the day, open your job in the
  2. media app (My jobs) and start the upload for today's session.
  3. Upload every raw photo and video from the session — don't cull or
  4. pre-select before uploading. Raw handoff means complete, unedited
  5. coverage; culling happens downstream in tagging, not here.
  6. Confirm each asset finishes uploading (the app marks it uploaded)
  7. before closing the app or putting your device away — a transfer
  8. left mid-upload doesn't count as handed off.
  9. Once every asset from the session is uploaded, the session moves
  10. to uploaded status and hands off to the tagging queue — this is
  11. what makes the day's photos visible to the tagging team.
  12. Each completed upload increments the day's photo-upload counter
  13. automatically; no manual count is needed.
  14. Retain your raw memory cards until you've confirmed the upload
  15. succeeded — don't format or reuse a card until the session shows
  16. uploaded.
  17. If the upload fails or stalls, notify the event lead right away
  18. rather than repeatedly retrying alone; they escalate to the
  19. director if it isn't resolved quickly. Keep the cards physically
  20. secure and retained until the upload is confirmed, however long
  21. that takes.
  22. Once the issue is resolved and the upload completes, confirm the
  23. counter reflects today's full session before considering handoff
  24. done.

Your day: post day

Photo publish

You own this

When: After tagging is complete (typically 24 hours)

If something goes wrong: If Photographer unreachable, Event Lead pings Director; publish authority can be re-assigned for that batch only.

  1. Confirm tagging is complete for the session — it should show
  2. "ready" in the tagging queue, meaning every asset worth keeping
  3. has been tagged to the right players or teams.
  4. Confirm the session's intended media-auth scope (internal,
  5. promotional, or public) is set correctly for how these photos are
  6. meant to be used — this determines whose consent the platform
  7. checks before allowing publish.
  8. Publishing a session (flipping it from ready to published) is an
  9. org-admin action in the admin media console — request that
  10. publish from whoever holds that access for your org (typically
  11. the director or office) rather than trying to do it yourself if
  12. you don't have admin login.
  13. When publish is attempted, the platform automatically checks every
  14. tagged family member against their media-authorization consent for
  15. the session's intended scope. If any tagged participant is missing
  16. consent for that scope, publish either hard-blocks with the list
  17. of missing names (if the org has consent enforcement turned on) or
  18. proceeds with a logged warning — either way, treat a soft-warn as
  19. unfinished business, not "handled," and follow up on the missing
  20. consent.
  21. If publish is blocked for missing consent, resolve the actual gap
  22. — confirm with the family, or drop that specific photo from the
  23. publish batch — rather than working around it by re-tagging around
  24. the flagged family members or loosening the intended scope to
  25. dodge the check.
  26. Once publish succeeds, each newly published asset increments the
  27. day's photos-published counter; no manual count is needed.
  28. Spot-check that published photos are visible where families expect
  29. to see them before considering the batch done.
  30. If you're unreachable to coordinate the publish request, the
  31. event lead pings the director, and publish authority for that
  32. batch can be reassigned per the escalation path — the consent
  33. check still applies regardless of who executes the publish.
