Rainout refund decision

You own this

When: 24 hours after a recorded rainout

If something goes wrong: If Director unreachable, Venue Manager may default to "credit only" until Director sign-off; refund disbursements still require Director authorization.

  1. Within 24 hours of a recorded rainout, pull the rainout decision
  2. record and the full list of registrations affected — every family
  3. registered for the cancelled or shortened match(es) at that
  4. venue/date.
  5. Decide the outcome using three tiers, ordered by how much of the
  6. session actually happened. If the match never started, default to
  7. reschedule where a make-up date is realistic within the season
  8. window — a family that showed up shouldn't lose the game outright
  9. if the calendar can absorb it. If a make-up date isn't realistic
  10. (end of season, no venue or ref availability), the call moves to
  11. refund or credit.
  12. Between refund and credit: refund returns money to the original
  13. payment method now; credit holds the value toward a future season
  14. or program and doesn't move cash today. Default to credit when the
  15. family is already registered for, or likely to register for, a
  16. future season; default to refund when they aren't. This is a
  17. policy call, not a formula — document the reasoning either way.
  18. Decide full vs. partial. A cancellation before kickoff is normally
  19. a full refund or credit of that game's value; a match stopped
  20. partway through (weather mid-game) is prorated at your discretion,
  21. with the proration basis documented in the rationale.
  22. Record the decision — outcome, rationale, affected registration
  23. count, full vs. partial — and your name as authorizing signer on
  24. the rainout refund decision form.
  25. For a refund outcome, action it through each affected
  26. registration's admin refund action (amount, reason, whether to
  27. also cancel the registration) individually — the form documents
  28. the policy call, but the money moves per registration, not as one
  29. batch transaction.
  30. For a credit outcome, there's no automated credit ledger yet —
  31. record the credit amount and reason on the form and hand off to
  32. the office to track it manually against that family's account
  33. until a dedicated credit ledger ships. Don't let a credit promise
  34. go untracked between the decision and its application.
  35. Submit the form. Family and team-captain notice of the outcome
  36. goes out through the platform per the standard notification
  37. policy — don't message families individually ahead of the form.
  38. If you're unreachable, the venue manager may default to "credit
  39. only" until you sign off per the escalation path — actual refund
  40. disbursement still waits for your authorization.

Rainout reschedule

You own this

When: 24 hours after a recorded rainout (when reschedule is the chosen path)

If something goes wrong: If Director unreachable, Venue Manager may propose reschedule candidates; Director sign-off still required before publish.

  1. Confirm reschedule is the chosen outcome from the rainout refund
  2. decision (or noted directly on the original rainout decision)
  3. before starting — this activity only runs when reschedule, not
  4. refund or credit, was picked.
  5. Identify make-up date candidates: check venue/field availability
  6. for open slots before the season ends, cross-check the ref
  7. assignment calendar for crew availability, and check the league
  8. schedule for conflicts (playoffs, tournament dates, other teams'
  9. existing games).
  10. Consult venue_manager on facility feasibility and event_lead on
  11. staffing/ref feasibility for each candidate date before committing
  12. — this is a three-way constraint (venue, refs, calendar), not just
  13. an open calendar slot.
  14. Prefer the earliest feasible date so a cancelled match doesn't
  15. compound into a scheduling backlog; if no single date works for
  16. both teams, consider splitting into separate make-up dates rather
  17. than delaying indefinitely.
  18. Record the chosen date(s), venue/field, and any conditions (e.g.,
  19. "conditional on ref availability") on the rainout reschedule form.
  20. Submit the form. This feeds the platform's rescheduling workflow,
  21. which updates the affected teams' schedules and triggers the
  22. standard notification to coaches, refs, captains, and parents —
  23. don't announce the date separately ahead of the form.
  24. If a chosen date conflicts with something noticed after the fact
  25. (a ref crew falls through, a field becomes unavailable), amend the
  26. reschedule promptly rather than letting stale info stand — a wrong
  27. make-up date reaching a parent is worse than a delayed one.
  28. If you're unreachable, the venue manager may propose candidate
  29. dates, but the reschedule doesn't publish until you sign off.

Weekly safety review

You own this

When: Weekly cadence after the weekly metrics rollup lands

If something goes wrong: If Director unreachable in a given week, Venue Manager drafts the review and Director signs off retroactively; no review goes unsigned past two weeks.

  1. After the weekly metrics rollup lands, pull the week's finalized
  2. incident reports, incident follow-ups, and field damage reports
  3. across every venue — this review works from the finalized record,
  4. not the in-the-moment incident response forms.
  5. Read every incident from the week, not just the serious ones — a
  6. pattern across several minor incidents (same field, same age
  7. group, same time of day) is exactly what this review exists to
  8. catch before it becomes a serious one.
  9. Note any near-miss or close call reported outside the formal
  10. incident process (a coach or venue manager flagging something in a
  11. debrief or in person) — near-misses count for this review even
  12. without their own form.
  13. Cross-reference the week's field damage reports for any pattern
  14. suggesting a facility issue rather than normal wear (recurring
  15. turf failure at the same spot, equipment failing at the same
  16. venue).
  17. For each theme identified, decide whether it needs a policy
  18. change, a facility repair or investment, additional training, or
  19. no action beyond documentation — record the decision and rationale,
  20. not just the observation.
  21. Consult the venue_manager on any decision affecting their venue's
  22. operations before finalizing — they're the one who executes most
  23. facility and procedural changes day to day.
  24. Record the week's themes, decisions, and any facility or policy
  25. changes on the weekly safety review form, and submit it.
  26. Any decision requiring facility investment or a policy change gets
  27. flagged explicitly for follow-through — don't let a documented
  28. decision sit undone; the next week's review should show it closed
  29. out or explain why not.
  30. If you're unavailable in a given week, the venue manager drafts
  31. the review and you sign off retroactively — no review goes
  32. unsigned past two weeks per the escalation path.

Safety & escalation

  • If Director unreachable in a given week, Venue Manager drafts the review and Director signs off retroactively; no review goes unsigned past two weeks.
  • If Director unreachable, Venue Manager may default to "credit only" until Director sign-off; refund disbursements still require Director authorization.
  • If Director unreachable, Venue Manager may propose reschedule candidates; Director sign-off still required before publish.
