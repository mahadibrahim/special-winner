Front of House

Training deck

Owns check-in, Parent comms, walk-on registrations, and concessions during an event-day.

Why this role matters

You are the first hello and the last impression. Registration, concessions, lost-and-found — families judge the whole organization by how you treat them.

What we believe

These four beliefs shape every practice plan, every sideline conversation, and every call we make about a kid.

Development Over Winning

We measure success by effort, improvement, and enjoyment — not the scoreboard. Winning takes care of itself when kids develop the right way.

Every Child Can Improve

There's no such thing as a "non-athletic" kid. With the right environment and encouragement, every child can grow their abilities — talent is built, not discovered.

What we believe (continued)

Long-Term Athlete Development

We're building athletes for age 25, not just age 8. We never trade away a child's long-term growth for a short-term result.

Holistic Growth

Sport develops the whole child — skills, game understanding, fitness, and confidence together. We pay attention to all of it, not just the physical side.

How that shows up day to day

We coach to two goals at once: compete to win, and grow every player's character and competence. When those two pull in different directions, character comes first.

Day to day, that means we praise effort, learning, and bouncing back from mistakes — the things a kid actually controls — over results they can't always control.

Whatever your role, this is what families should feel from us.

Concession inventory count (open)

You own this

When: After concession setup, before first sale

If something goes wrong: If Front of House unreachable, escalate to Venue Manager per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. Do this right after concession setup finishes and before the stand
  2. opens for its first sale — counting after sales have started
  3. makes the numbers useless for reconciliation.
  4. Count drinks by type and size, working from the front cooler back
  5. to the stockroom so nothing gets double-counted.
  6. Count snacks and packaged goods by SKU.
  7. Count hot-food pars — the prepped starting quantity for items made
  8. in batches (hot dogs, pretzels), not raw ingredient stock.
  9. Count paper goods and consumables that affect per-unit cost
  10. tracking: cups, napkins, condiment packets.
  11. Enter every count into the inventory form by item — don't batch
  12. categories together, since end-of-day reconciliation matches this
  13. count line-by-line against the closing count.
  14. Note anything already damaged, short-dated, or missing from the
  15. expected opening stock before the first sale, and flag it to the
  16. venue manager if it looks like a shrink or delivery problem.
  17. Submit the form. This is what the cash and concession reconcile
  18. compares against at the end of the day.

Concession setup

You own this

When: About 90 minutes before first kickoff (concessions venues only)

There's a checklist for this — see the checklist slides.

If something goes wrong: If Front of House unreachable, escalate to Venue Manager per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. About 90 minutes before first kickoff, power on all concession
  2. equipment — fountain machine, warmers, grill/hot-food equipment,
  3. registers — and give it time to reach operating temperature before
  4. you need it.
  5. Restock the front cooler and display cases from the back cooler so
  6. the stand looks fully stocked when families arrive.
  7. Set up the POS terminal: confirm it's connected, logged in under
  8. today's shift, and the opening cash bank (the fixed starting
  9. drawer amount) is in the drawer and matches the expected amount.
  10. Calibrate fountain syrup-to-water ratios and taste-test each flavor
  11. before opening — a flat or over-syrupy fountain is the most common
  12. concession complaint.
  13. Confirm hand-wash stations are stocked with soap and paper towels,
  14. and sanitizer stations/wipes are stocked at the register and food
  15. prep area.
  16. Check food safety basics: cold items are cold, hot items are hot,
  17. and any date-sensitive stock (dairy, prepped items) is within
  18. date.
  19. Do a final walk of the stand for anything blocking service — boxes
  20. in the walkway, signage not yet up, menu board not updated for
  21. today's pricing.
  22. Once the stand is fully ready, mark the checklist complete — this
  23. clears the way for the concession inventory count.

Walk-on registration

You own this

When: Throughout the drop-in/clinic intake window

If something goes wrong: If Front of House unreachable, escalate to Event Lead per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. Throughout the drop-in/clinic intake window, greet each walk-on
  2. parent or adult player at the front-of-house station — the coach
  3. running the session isn't the one who registers walk-ons.
  4. Open walk-up registration and collect the player/adult info: name,
  5. date of birth (youth) or the adult registrant's own info, and an
  6. emergency contact and phone number.
  7. Note any medical or allergy information the coach should know
  8. before the player joins the session.
  9. Select the correct session/program the walk-on is joining so
  10. they're added to the right roster, not just "today's activity."
  11. Capture payment before the player joins play — record the payment
  12. status and method; if paying at the desk in person, mark it paid
  13. once the payment is actually taken.
  14. Get the liability waiver signed. In person, that's the paper
  15. waiver at the desk; note it as signed once collected — never let a
  16. player join on a verbal "I'll sign later."
  17. Add the player to the session roster so the coach's headcount and
  18. ratio check reflects them before they join.
  19. Once registration, payment, and waiver are all complete, the
  20. walk-on counter increments automatically — confirm the player is
  21. on the roster the coach sees before sending them to the field.
  22. If intake volume would push a session over its coach ratio cap,
  23. tell the coach to close intake for that session and hold
  24. additional walk-ons for the next available slot.

Spectator management

You own this

When: Spectator complaint or sideline issue raised during a match

If something goes wrong: If issue exceeds Front of House scope, escalate to Event Lead; any safety-relevant escalation goes to Venue Manager.

  1. When a spectator complaint or sideline issue is raised — a seating
  2. conflict, a lost parent, a behavior concern — respond in person
  3. rather than trying to resolve it by shouting across the sideline.
  4. Get the reporting spectator's account first, without interrupting
  5. the match in progress unless the issue is safety-relevant.
  6. For seat conflicts or general confusion (wrong bleacher section,
  7. can't find their kid's field), resolve on the spot — this doesn't
  8. need the form unless it escalates.
  9. For behavior issues (a heated spectator, unsporting conduct from
  10. the sideline, a dispute between parents), speak with the person
  11. directly, calmly, and away from the match.
  12. If the person won't de-escalate or the issue is safety-relevant
  13. (physical altercation, threats), pull in the event lead
  14. immediately rather than continuing to handle it alone.
  15. Any safety-relevant issue also goes to the venue manager right
  16. away, in parallel with pulling in the event lead — don't wait for
  17. one before notifying the other.
  18. Log the complaint details in the form regardless of outcome: what
  19. happened, who was involved, how it was resolved. Even minor,
  20. fully-resolved issues get logged — this is what surfaces repeat
  21. problem patterns over time.
  22. Submit the form within about 5 minutes of the issue arising, while
  23. the details are still fresh.

Cash and concession reconcile

You own this

When: After concession close, before deposit

If something goes wrong: If Front of House unreachable, Venue Manager performs the reconciliation; any cash variance over threshold escalates to Director.

  1. Close the concession stand to new sales before starting the count —
  2. don't count a drawer that's still taking cash.
  3. Count the drawer cash by denomination with a second person present
  4. as witness; the witness watches the count, they don't just take
  5. your word for the total.
  6. Subtract the opening bank (the fixed starting cash noted at
  7. concession setup) from the counted total to get cash sales.
  8. Run the POS Z-report for the day and record card sales and total
  9. POS-recorded sales separately from the cash count.
  10. Count closing concession inventory against the opening inventory
  11. count to compute units sold by item.
  12. Multiply units sold by price to get expected revenue, then compare
  13. expected revenue to (cash sales + card sales) and record the
  14. variance, over or short.
  15. If the variance is inside the normal tolerance, both you and the
  16. witness sign the reconciliation form and prepare the deposit.
  17. If the variance is outside tolerance, recount before assuming
  18. shrink or a counting error — then record the variance as-is and
  19. escalate to the venue manager; anything still unexplained after
  20. recount escalates to the director.
  21. File the signed, witnessed form with today's records before making
  22. the deposit.

Lost and found inventory

You own this

When: At end of day after the venue clears

If something goes wrong: If Front of House unreachable, Event Lead inventories and escalates to Venue Manager per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. After the venue clears at end of day, do one final sweep of common
  2. areas — bleachers, sidelines, locker rooms, restrooms, concession
  3. seating — for anything left behind, in addition to whatever's
  4. already in the lost-and-found bin.
  5. For each item, log a description specific enough to identify it
  6. (color, brand, size) — "water bottle" isn't enough on a day with
  7. six lost water bottles.
  8. Log where it was found (field/court, bleachers, restroom, parking
  9. lot) so a family calling in has a starting point.
  10. If a name, phone number, or team is visible on the item itself,
  11. log that contact info directly against the entry.
  12. If someone already reported the item missing today and you can
  13. match it, note the match and reach out with the contact info you
  14. have rather than waiting for them to call the office.
  15. Store the physical items together in the lost-and-found holding
  16. area, not scattered across storage.
  17. Submit the inventory form — this is what the office uses to match
  18. items to inquiries and to know what's still eligible for the
  19. holding-window disposal.
  20. If front of house isn't available to run this at close, the event
  21. lead does the inventory and escalates per the escalation path.
