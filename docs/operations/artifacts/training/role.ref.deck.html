Referee check-in

You're part of this

When: About 30 minutes before each kickoff

If something goes wrong: If Event Lead unreachable, escalate to Venue Manager per the standard handoff ladder; backup Referee pulled from standby pool if scheduled Referee no-shows.

  1. About 30 minutes before their assigned kickoff, the ref reports to
  2. the event lead's check-in station — the same match shows up in the
  3. ref's My Matches list on their phone.
  4. Confirm the ref's identity against today's confirmed assignment
  5. from the ref assignment confirm.
  6. Check the ref is in proper uniform (kit, badge/credential if
  7. required) and has their equipment — whistle, cards, watch.
  8. Confirm the ref knows their match: teams, field/court, kickoff
  9. time, and any special notes from the pregame weather or facility
  10. check.
  11. The ref signs in on the spot. This is what starts the stipend
  12. clock, so don't let a ref skip it and go straight to the field.
  13. Note the actual check-in time — if it's inside the 30-minute
  14. window but tight, give the ref a heads-up on time remaining before
  15. kickoff.
  16. If the scheduled ref doesn't show by check-in time, pull the backup
  17. from the standby pool immediately and check them in instead —
  18. don't wait past the window hoping the original ref appears.
  19. If the event lead is unavailable to run check-in, escalate to the
  20. venue manager per the escalation path.

Live score update

You own this

When: Each scoring event during the match

If something goes wrong: If the entry app fails, Referee keeps a paper tally and Event Lead enters the running score in batch; escalate to Venue Manager if the issue persists across matches.

  1. Throughout the match, keep your own live tally of the score as
  2. goals happen — who scored, which side, roughly what minute. This
  3. is the authoritative running score until it's entered into the
  4. system at the final report.
  5. If the venue has a manual scoreboard or signage table, keep it
  6. updated goal by goal so spectators and any parents following along
  7. see the score change in real time. There's no in-app live-score
  8. screen yet (real-time phone entry is still on the roadmap), so this
  9. is how "live" actually reaches spectators today.
  10. At each stoppage — end of a period or half, an injury break —
  11. cross-check your tally against your assistant ref's count and the
  12. venue scoreboard if one is running, so a missed goal doesn't carry
  13. through to the end of the match.
  14. If the event lead or a parent asks for the current score mid-match,
  15. you should be able to answer immediately and with confidence, not
  16. have to go check anything.
  17. If your tally and your assistant's disagree, stop at the next dead
  18. ball and reconcile with the two captains/coaches before play
  19. resumes — sorting this out live is far easier than reconstructing
  20. it after the final whistle.
  21. Reconcile your running tally one more time at the final whistle
  22. before you go to file the match report. The number you enter as
  23. the final score should match what you and your assistant have been
  24. tracking all match, not a guess.
  25. If the count becomes genuinely unclear and can't be resolved on the
  26. field, flag it to the event lead immediately rather than guessing —
  27. the event lead escalates to the venue manager if it can't be
  28. resolved before the match needs to continue.
  29. Once the final report is submitted, that entry is what the
  30. platform's score and standings pipeline treats as the record of the
  31. match's scoring — there's no separate live feed to reconcile it
  32. against today.

Final score attestation

You own this

When: Final whistle of each match

If something goes wrong: If Referee unable to sign, Event Lead captures a co-attestation with the Referee's verbal confirmation and escalates to Venue Manager for any contested score.

  1. At the final whistle, open the match from My Matches — the same
  2. match that showed up at check-in — and go to its match report.
  3. Enter the final score for both teams in the Home score and Away
  4. score fields. Double-check the number against your own running
  5. tally (and your assistant's, if you have one) from the live score
  6. update before typing it in.
  7. Log every incident from the match — yellow cards, red cards,
  8. injuries, anything else notable — in the incidents list. If you
  9. already separately logged an ejection, list it here too; the match
  10. report is the complete record on its own, not a supplement to
  11. another log.
  12. Add anything else worth noting in the match notes field — a
  13. weather delay, a contested call, an early stoppage, anything that
  14. isn't captured by the score or the incident list.
  15. Review the score and incident list once more before submitting.
  16. There's no separate signature step — tapping Submit report is your
  17. attestation that this is the official, final result.
  18. Submit the report and confirm you see the saved confirmation
  19. before you leave the field or court. Don't walk away assuming it
  20. went through.
  21. Once submitted, the match is marked completed and this is the
  22. signed record the score post to standings activity picks up —
  23. treated as authoritative for any later dispute. Get it right the
  24. first time rather than planning to fix it afterward.
  25. If you're unable to submit yourself (injury, emergency, phone
  26. failure), the event lead captures your verbal confirmation of the
  27. score and incidents and files a co-attestation on your behalf per
  28. the escalation path.
  29. If a coach or team disputes the score on the spot, note the
  30. dispute in the match notes before submitting and flag it to the
  31. venue manager — don't hold up submission trying to resolve the
  32. dispute yourself.

Timekeeping

You own this

When: Match clock start at kickoff

If something goes wrong: If primary Referee unable to operate clock, the assistant Referee or event lead takes over; escalate to Venue Manager only if the match must be paused.

  1. Start the match clock at kickoff using your own watch or
  2. stopwatch — there's no in-app clock to start. Track time against
  3. the format's standard structure (halves/quarters/periods) for the
  4. sport you're officiating.
  5. Run the clock exactly as your sport/format's rules describe
  6. (running clock vs. stop clock, halftime length), call stoppage or
  7. added time as usual, and blow the final whistle when regulation and
  8. any added time expire.
  9. Note the actual kickoff time and final whistle time — if the match
  10. started late (delayed team, weather hold) or ran long (stoppage
  11. time, an injury delay), you'll want these when you fill out the
  12. match report.
  13. There's no separate clock-start/clock-stop button in the system
  14. today. What it actually captures is your check-in time and your
  15. final-report submission time as the proxy for kickoff and final
  16. whistle — so an accurate check-in and a prompt final-report
  17. submission are what keep the match record's elapsed time close to
  18. what really happened on the field.
  19. If the match ran unusually long or short of its scheduled duration
  20. (called early for weather, extended for a delay), say so in the
  21. match notes on the final report. That's what the office, and any
  22. stipend or time dispute review, will check against.
  23. If you're unable to keep time yourself mid-match (injury, needing
  24. to step away), hand it to your assistant ref; if none is
  25. available, ask the event lead to keep visible time from the
  26. sideline until you're back.
  27. If play has to stop for something beyond a normal stoppage (medical
  28. emergency, weather), note the stoppage length so the resumed clock
  29. and the final report reflect actual playing time. Escalate to the
  30. venue manager only if the match itself needs to be paused or
  31. abandoned.
  32. Submit the final report as promptly as possible after the final
  33. whistle — the gap between your actual final whistle and when the
  34. report lands is exactly the kind of drift timekeeping exists to
  35. keep small.

Ejection logging

You own this

When: Ejection issued during or immediately after the match

If something goes wrong: If Referee cannot complete the form, Event Lead captures the Referee's verbal account and files; escalate to Director for any ejection carrying multi-match suspension.

  1. When you issue an ejection — sending off a player, or ejecting a
  2. coach from the sideline — note the exact time, who was ejected
  3. (player number/name, or "Coach"), and the reason immediately.
  4. Memory of exact wording fades fast once play resumes.
  5. If play can continue, don't stop to file anything on the spot —
  6. finish officiating the match. The ejection gets logged as part of
  7. the match report you file at the final whistle (final score
  8. attestation).
  9. On the match report, log the ejection as an incident: type "Red
  10. card" for a player sent off (the closest match to an ejection in
  11. today's incident types), the side, the player/number or "COACH" if
  12. it was a coach, the minute, and a description that spells out the
  13. reason — and for a coach, states plainly that it was a coach
  14. ejection, not a player card.
  15. If the ejection carries a suspension under your league's rules
  16. (e.g., a red card that sits a player for the next match), say so
  17. explicitly in the incident description — the office relies on this
  18. note to catch the suspension, since there's no separate suspension
  19. field yet.
  20. For an ejected coach, also flag it directly to the event lead in
  21. person before you leave — the report captures the record, but the
  22. event lead needs to know in the moment in case the coach needs to
  23. be walked off site.
  24. Submit the match report as usual. This is what files the ejection
  25. into the incident log, alongside the score and every other
  26. incident from the match.
  27. If you can't complete the report yourself (injury, emergency), give
  28. the event lead your verbal account of what happened — who, what,
  29. when, why — and have them file it on your behalf per the
  30. escalation path.
  31. Any ejection you believe could carry a multi-match suspension gets
  32. escalated to the director in addition to being logged — don't
  33. count on the office catching it from the incident description
  34. alone.

Safety & escalation

  • If Event Lead unreachable, escalate to Venue Manager per the standard handoff ladder; backup Referee pulled from standby pool if scheduled Referee no-shows.
  • If Referee cannot complete the form, Event Lead captures the Referee's verbal account and files; escalate to Director for any ejection carrying multi-match suspension.
  • If Referee unable to sign, Event Lead captures a co-attestation with the Referee's verbal confirmation and escalates to Venue Manager for any contested score.
  • If primary Referee unable to operate clock, the assistant Referee or event lead takes over; escalate to Venue Manager only if the match must be paused.
  • If the entry app fails, Referee keeps a paper tally and Event Lead enters the running score in batch; escalate to Venue Manager if the issue persists across matches.
