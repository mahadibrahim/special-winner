Your job

You run one team's sessions from open to close. You start and end each class or camp session, rotate group activities during camp play, and support the pregame briefing before youth league matches. Attendance, conduct, and compliance for your team run through you.

How the day flows for you: pre game, in game, and post game.

What you're responsible for

  • Pre game: class/camp session open
  • In game: camp group rotation
  • Post game: class/camp session close

What we believe

These four beliefs shape every practice plan, every sideline conversation, and every call we make about a kid.

Development Over Winning

We measure success by effort, improvement, and enjoyment — not the scoreboard. Winning takes care of itself when kids develop the right way.

Every Child Can Improve

There's no such thing as a "non-athletic" kid. With the right environment and encouragement, every child can grow their abilities — talent is built, not discovered.

06 / 37

What we believe (continued)

Long-Term Athlete Development

We're building athletes for age 25, not just age 8. We never trade away a child's long-term growth for a short-term result.

Holistic Growth

Sport develops the whole child — skills, game understanding, fitness, and confidence together. We pay attention to all of it, not just the physical side.

07 / 37

How that shows up day to day

We coach to two goals at once: compete to win, and grow every player's character and competence. When those two pull in different directions, character comes first.

Day to day, that means we praise effort, learning, and bouncing back from mistakes — the things a kid actually controls — over results they can't always control.

Whatever your role, this is what families should feel from us.

10 / 37

Class/camp session open (1/2)

  1. Arrive early enough to finish this checklist before players are dropped off. Pull today's roster in /coach/attendance.
  2. Walk the playing space: wet spots, debris, damaged fixtures, and any goal on the space — confirm it's anchored or counterweighted, never propped loose. Report anything unsafe to the venue manager before setup. Players never move or hang on a goal, at any age.
  3. Stage equipment for the first activity: age-appropriate balls, cones, pinnies. Confirm a first-aid kit is at the field/court.
  4. Greet players as they arrive and mark attendance against the roster. For clinics/drop-ins, only players registered by front of house (walk-on registration) may join.
WhenAbout 20 minutes before the scheduled session start
AccountabilityYou own this
ChecklistClass session open

Tools

  • The attendance roster (/coach/attendance)
  • First-aid kit (on-site)
  • Age-appropriate balls, cones, and pinnies
11 / 37

Class/camp session open (2/2)

  1. Before play begins, check headcount against the staffing ratio and class cap for the age group — ages 4-6: 1 coach per 4 players, class of 8-10 max; ages 7-9: 1:5, 10-12 max; ages 10-12: 1:6, 12-14 max. For drop-in/clinic sessions, re-check as walk-ons join during the intake window.
  2. If over-ratio, hold the start and escalate per the escalation path.
If something goes wrong: If headcount exceeds the ratio cap (e.g., a Coach no-show), the Coach holds the start and escalates directly to Venue Manager per the Coach → Venue Manager → Director handoff ladder. A session never starts over-ratio.
12 / 37

Coach pregame briefing (youth league) (1/3)

  1. This runs for youth-league matches only, alongside team check-in, about 15 minutes before kickoff — adult leagues are self-managed and skip it entirely.
  2. Gather both teams' coaches together, not one at a time, so both hear the same information.
  3. Cover rules of the day: any format quirks (small-sided rules, quarter/half length for the age group), and anything from the coach pregame dispatch packet worth repeating out loud.
  4. Cover special situations: weather watch status if outdoor, any field/court condition notes, rescheduled or shortened matches.
WhenAbout 15 minutes before each youth-league kickoff
AccountabilityYou assist

Tools

  • The coach pregame briefing dispatch packet (reference for rules-of-the-day)
  • Printed sign-in sheet (coach acknowledgment)
  • Today's weather/facility check notes (special-situations reference)
13 / 37

Coach pregame briefing (youth league) (2/3)

  1. Restate conduct expectations — sideline coaching by parents, unsporting conduct, and that the code of conduct enforcement activity is what handles violations, not an on-field argument.
  2. Introduce the ref crew for the match if the coaches haven't already met them at this venue.
  3. Ask if either coach has a conflict to flag before kickoff (a player who needs to leave early, an injury from warmups).
  4. Each coach signs to acknowledge the briefing.

  1. If the event lead can't run the briefing, escalate to the venue manager per the escalation path rather than skipping it.
If something goes wrong: If Event Lead unreachable, escalate to Venue Manager per the Coach → Venue Manager → Director handoff ladder.

Camp group rotation (1/2)

  1. Two minutes before the scheduled transition, wind down the current activity and gather the group (transitions are listed on the camp daily schedule, e.g., full-day blocks like 9:45-10:45 technical session, 11:00-11:45 small-sided games).
  2. Collect any equipment that belongs to the current station; carry group gear (water cooler, clipboard/roster) with the group.
  3. Walk the group to the next station together — no child moves between stations alone.
  4. At the new station, count heads against the group roster before play resumes. Ages 4-6 groups are 8-10 per coach, ages 7-9 are 10-12, ages 10-12 are 12-14 — know your expected number before you move.
WhenAt each scheduled activity-block transition on the camp daily schedule
AccountabilityYou own this

Tools

  • Group roster on a clipboard (headcount reference at each station)
  • Water cooler carried with the group
  • Camp daily schedule (station transition times)
17 / 37

Camp group rotation (2/2)

  1. If the count matches, increment the rotation counter and start the next activity. If it does not, stop and escalate per the escalation path.
  2. For ages 4-6, plan activity changes every 8-10 minutes within a station; the roster headcount is only required at station moves.
If something goes wrong: If headcount does not match the group roster, all play stops immediately and the Coach escalates directly to Venue Manager per the Coach → Venue Manager → Director handoff ladder; the search for the missing child takes priority over every other activity on site.

Class/camp session close (1/2)

  1. End the session on time and on a positive note (cooldown, review, at-home challenge per the session structure).
  2. Release each player only to a recognized parent/guardian. No child leaves unaccompanied. Stay with any un-picked-up player and escalate per the escalation path.
  3. Use pickup as the parent handoff: one sentence on what the session worked on and the at-home challenge (keep it fun and brief, 10-15 min).
  4. Tear down: count balls, cones, pinnies, and portable goals back into storage. Flag missing or damaged equipment to facilities.
  5. Sweep the playing area for personal items; anything found goes to lost and found.
WhenAt the scheduled end of the session
AccountabilityYou own this
ChecklistClass session close

Tools

  • The coach portal — attendance and session notes (/coach/attendance)
  • Storage bins for balls, cones, pinnies, and portable goals
  • Lost-and-found bin

Class/camp session close (2/2)

  1. Finalize attendance and log coach notes in the coach portal while the session is fresh. Leave the space ready for the next group.
If something goes wrong: If a Player has no Parent/guardian at pickup, the Coach stays with the Player and escalates directly to Venue Manager per the Coach → Venue Manager → Director handoff ladder. The Coach never leaves before the last Player has been picked up.

Class session close

  • Every player released to a recognized parent/guardian (no child leaves unaccompanied)
  • Parents told what the session worked on and given the at-home challenge
  • Equipment counted and returned to storage (balls, cones, pinnies, portable goals)
  • Playing area swept for personal items; anything found goes to lost and found
  • Coach notes / attendance finalized in the coach portal
  • Space left ready for the next group (trash cleared, fixtures back in place)
23 / 37

Class session open

  • Today's roster and attendance sheet open in /coach/attendance
  • Playing space walked for hazards (wet spots, debris, damaged fixtures, any goal anchored/counterweighted)
  • Session equipment staged (balls sized for the age group, cones, pinnies)
  • First-aid kit present at the field/court
  • Attendance recorded against the roster
  • Headcount vs. staffing within program ratio and class cap (ages 4-6: 1:4, 8-10 max; ages 7-9: 1:5, 10-12 max; ages 10-12: 1:6, 12-14 max)
