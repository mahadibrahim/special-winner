Venue Manager

Training deck

Single venue, single day. The on-site decision-maker accountable for the end-to-end execution of an event-day at one location.

Why this role matters

You make the whole day work. From unlock to lock-up, every family's experience runs through the venue you run — smooth, safe, end to end.

What we believe

These four beliefs shape every practice plan, every sideline conversation, and every call we make about a kid.

Development Over Winning

We measure success by effort, improvement, and enjoyment — not the scoreboard. Winning takes care of itself when kids develop the right way.

Every Child Can Improve

There's no such thing as a "non-athletic" kid. With the right environment and encouragement, every child can grow their abilities — talent is built, not discovered.

What we believe (continued)

Long-Term Athlete Development

We're building athletes for age 25, not just age 8. We never trade away a child's long-term growth for a short-term result.

Holistic Growth

Sport develops the whole child — skills, game understanding, fitness, and confidence together. We pay attention to all of it, not just the physical side.

How that shows up day to day

We coach to two goals at once: compete to win, and grow every player's character and competence. When those two pull in different directions, character comes first.

Day to day, that means we praise effort, learning, and bouncing back from mistakes — the things a kid actually controls — over results they can't always control.

Whatever your role, this is what families should feel from us.

Staff schedule confirmation

You own this

When: 48 hours before the event

There's a checklist for this — see the checklist slides.

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, escalate to Director per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. 48 hours before the event window, pull the day's staffing plan and
  2. list every shift slot that needs to be covered — venue manager,
  3. event leads, facilities, front of house, and photographer.
  4. Check the acknowledgment status the platform has already collected
  5. for each assigned person (the schedule invite went out automatically
  6. when the plan was published).
  7. For any slot still unacknowledged, contact the assigned person
  8. directly and get a yes/no before moving on to the next slot.
  9. For any no-response or decline, pull a replacement from that role's
  10. standby pool and confirm their availability for the full shift.
  11. Watch for double-booked people or shift times that don't cover the
  12. full event window, and fix the gap before confirming the slot.
  13. Confirm contractor slots (ref, photographer) separately — they come
  14. from the assignor/contractor pool, not the staff schedule, and need
  15. their own acknowledgment check.
  16. Once every slot on the plan shows a confirmed name, mark the
  17. checklist complete.
  18. If any role still can't be filled by T-24h, escalate per the
  19. escalation path rather than running the event short-staffed.

Pre-day weather pre-check

You own this

When: 72 hours before the event for outdoor venues (scheduled review)

There's a checklist for this — see the checklist slides.

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, escalate to Director per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. 72 hours before the event window, pull up every outdoor match on the
  2. day's schedule and check the forecast for temperature, precipitation
  3. chance, wind, and lightning risk on the weather-alert dashboard.
  4. Flag a heat advisory if the forecast heat index reaches 95°F or
  5. higher during the event window, and flag a precipitation risk if the
  6. chance of rain is 60% or higher during that window.
  7. If the surface is accessible, walk it and note current field
  8. condition (standing water from recent rain, firmness) as a baseline.
  9. If nothing is flagged, complete the checklist — no further action is
  10. needed until the pregame recheck 90 minutes before kickoff.
  11. If something is flagged, notify the event lead and the director
  12. right away so staffing and family communications can adjust early,
  13. and schedule a follow-up recheck at T-24h before the forecast locks
  14. in.
  15. Re-run the T-24h recheck if step 5 applied, and carry any still-open
  16. risk forward into the pregame weather check.

Facility unlock

You own this

When: About 2 hours before first kickoff

There's a checklist for this — see the checklist slides.

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, escalate to Director per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. Arrive at least 2 hours before first kickoff — before any other
  2. staff.
  3. Disarm the alarm with the current code; if the code fails, use the
  4. backup entry procedure and log the discrepancy.
  5. Unlock the exterior doors staff and families will need today (main
  6. entrance, staff entrance, any concessions or loading doors) per the
  7. day's traffic plan.
  8. Unlock interior doors: locker rooms, storage/equipment room,
  9. concessions, restrooms, office.
  10. Turn on lights throughout the facility and set HVAC to the
  11. comfortable operating range for the season.
  12. Confirm restrooms are functioning — running water, no leaks, no
  13. overnight plumbing issues.
  14. Note anything unusual from overnight (forced-entry signs, alarm
  15. trouble codes, standing water) and escalate per the escalation path
  16. before staff arrive.
  17. Mark the checklist complete once the building is ready for staff —
  18. this clears the way for the opening walkthrough.

First-aid kit check

You own this

When: About 60 minutes before first kickoff

There's a checklist for this — see the checklist slides.

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, escalate to Director per the standard handoff ladder; no event begins without a verified kit.

  1. Open every first-aid kit on premises — one per field/court plus any
  2. building kit.
  3. Check contents against the kit manifest: bandages, gauze, tape,
  4. antiseptic wipes, gloves, cold packs, CPR mask.
  5. Restock any missing or expired item from bulk supply before moving
  6. to the next kit.
  7. Locate the AED and confirm it's in its wall mount or designated
  8. spot.
  9. Check the AED's battery indicator and confirm the pads are within
  10. their expiration date; swap in fresh pads from backup stock if
  11. they've expired.
  12. Confirm each kit's location is known to today's coaches and event
  13. leads — a kit at the field/court is one of the items on their own
  14. session-open check.
  15. Log the check complete. No event begins without a verified kit — if
  16. any kit or the AED can't be verified, escalate immediately per the
  17. escalation path and hold the start of play at that surface.

Opening walkthrough

You own this

When: Immediately after facility unlock

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, escalate to Director per the standard handoff ladder; Facilities walks the building until coverage resumes.

  1. Immediately after unlock, walk the entire facility in a fixed route:
  2. playing surfaces, restrooms, locker rooms, bleachers/spectator
  3. areas, then the parking lot.
  4. At each surface, look for standing water, debris, damaged fixtures
  5. (goals, nets, benches, lighting), and anything a player or spectator
  6. could trip on or be hurt by.
  7. Check restrooms and locker rooms for cleanliness, supplies (soap,
  8. paper towels or toilet paper), and any plumbing issues.
  9. Check bleachers and spectator areas for structural issues (loose
  10. bolts, damaged seating) and general cleanliness.
  11. Check the parking lot for surface hazards and lighting, and confirm
  12. the overflow path is clear.
  13. Record every finding in the walkthrough form with location and
  14. severity, even minor ones — this is the day's baseline safety
  15. record.
  16. Route anything unsafe to facilities for correction before players
  17. and spectators arrive; anything that can't be resolved before the
  18. event escalates per the escalation path.
  19. Submit the completed form before the pre-shift staff briefing so
  20. findings can be shared with the team.

  4. Keep it to 5-10 minutes. Cover today's schedule (match count, key

  8. escalation path).
  9. Call out any staffing changes from the schedule confirm — who's
  10. covering for a standby swap, who's new to the venue today.
  11. Ask if anyone has a conflict or needs to leave early, and confirm
  12. coverage for that window now rather than discovering it mid-shift.
  13. Pass around the sign-in sheet — every attendee signs to confirm
  14. they were briefed.
  15. Front of house checks the sign-in sheet against today's staffing
  16. plan and signs to confirm the full roster attended and the sheet is
  17. complete.
  18. File the signed sheet with today's event records.
  19. If the venue manager can't deliver the briefing, the event lead
  20. runs it and escalates per the escalation path.

Field condition photo (pregame)

You own this

When: About 30 minutes before first kickoff on each surface

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, Facilities captures the photo and escalates to Director per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. About 30 minutes before the first match on each surface, walk to
  2. that surface with a phone or camera.
  3. Frame a wide shot showing the full playing surface — turf/grass
  4. condition, lines, and goals or nets in position.
  5. Take the photo with location and timestamp on so it's date-stamped
  6. automatically.
  7. Upload it to the day's match record under that surface before the
  8. first whistle.
  9. Repeat for every surface hosting a match that day — one dated photo
  10. per surface, before its first kickoff.
  11. If the photo reveals a condition issue (standing water, divots,
  12. damaged line paint), flag it to facilities right away rather than
  13. waiting for the field damage process.
  14. Don't skip this even on a clear day — it's the baseline any later
  15. damage claim gets measured against.

Rainout decision

You own this

When: Weather/field condition within 2 hours of kickoff suggests cancellation

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, escalate to Director who has unilateral authority to call the event.

  1. When weather or field conditions within 2 hours of kickoff raise
  2. real doubt — active lightning, sustained heavy rain producing
  3. standing water or unsafe footing, or a heat index at or above the
  4. venue's cutoff — pull the current weather-alert dashboard reading
  5. and the field condition status.
  6. Consult the director before making the call. This is a consequential
  7. decision; if the director can't be reached in time, the decision
  8. defaults to them per the escalation path.
  9. For lightning specifically, use the standard flash-to-bang rule:
  10. suspend play if thunder follows lightning within 30 seconds, and
  11. don't resume until 30 minutes have passed with no further lightning.
  12. Decide one of three outcomes: play as scheduled, delay the start
  13. (name a re-check time), or cancel.
  14. Record the decision, the specific conditions that drove it, and your
  15. name as authorizing signer in the rainout decision form.
  16. Submit the form. This triggers the platform's cancellation broadcast
  17. to parents, coaches, refs, captains, and the venue team
  18. automatically — don't message people separately ahead of the form.
  19. If the outcome is cancel, remember the refund/reschedule call
  20. (rainout refund decision, rainout reschedule) belongs to the
  21. director within the next 24 hours — your job ends at the call and
  22. the record.

You own this

When: About 90 minutes before kickoff (outdoor venues only)

There's a checklist for this — see the checklist slides.

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, escalate to Director per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. About 90 minutes before kickoff at any outdoor venue, re-check the
  2. weather-alert dashboard for live conditions — this replaces the 72h
  3. pre-check with real-time data.
  4. Check the lightning radar for detected strikes within 10 miles of
  5. the venue.
  6. Check current precipitation on radar and confirm whether it's
  7. arriving before, during, or after the event window.
  8. Walk the surface (or have facilities confirm) for standing water,
  9. saturated turf, or other unsafe footing from overnight or same-day
  10. rain.
  11. Check current temperature and heat index against the venue's
  12. heat-advisory cutoff.
  13. If any reading crosses a risk threshold — active lightning, heavy
  14. rain producing standing water, a heat advisory — move straight to
  15. the rainout decision rather than waiting out the clock.
  16. If conditions are clear, complete the checklist and log the readings
  17. for the record; that's the data trail if conditions change later and
  18. the call gets challenged.

Incident response (in-the-moment)

You own this

When: Incident observed or reported during the match

If something goes wrong: Any life-threatening incident escalates immediately to 911 and Director; Venue Manager remains accountable for documentation but Event Lead may capture the initial form.

  1. The moment an incident is reported to you — injury, altercation,
  2. medical event, or property damage — get to the scene. If you
  3. didn't witness it yourself, find whoever did (coach, ref, event
  4. lead) before writing anything down.
  5. Scene safety and care come before documentation. If it's
  6. life-threatening, call 911 immediately — this also escalates
  7. directly to the director; don't wait to finish a form first.
  8. Never leave an injured or distressed child unattended while you
  9. sort out logistics.
  10. Get first-hand information from whoever responded: who was
  11. involved, what immediate care was given, whether 911 was called
  12. and when, and the exact time.
  13. Open the incident response form and capture what you have within
  14. about 5 minutes of the incident — this is the in-the-moment
  15. record, not the full report. It doesn't need to be complete, it
  16. needs to be fast and accurate: what happened, who responded, what
  17. care was given, whether 911 was called.
  18. If a parent is on-site, notify them directly and in person before
  19. they hear about it secondhand. Stick to what happened and what was
  20. done — don't speculate about diagnosis or fault.
  21. Handle this discreetly — this record names a minor. Don't discuss
  22. specifics in a group chat, in front of other families, or with
  23. anyone who doesn't need to know; the incident form is the record,
  24. not a text thread.
  25. Submit the form as soon as the immediate situation is stable. This
  26. starts the clock on the incident report finalization, which
  27. happens once the affected match is over.
  28. If you can't capture this yourself, the event lead may capture
  29. the initial form from the same first-hand accounts — you remain
  30. accountable for it either way.
  31. This form is the fast first pass, not the final record used for
  32. disputes or insurance — every incident captured here still needs
  33. the full finalized report before it's closed.

Incident report finalization

You own this

When: After the incident is stable and the affected match is over

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, Event Lead drafts and Director finalizes; nothing closes until the report is signed off.

  1. Once the affected match is over and the situation is stable, pull
  2. up the in-the-moment incident response form as your starting point
  3. — this report builds on it, it doesn't replace it.
  4. Identify every witness — players, coaches, refs, spectators who
  5. saw what happened — and get a brief statement from each while
  6. memories are fresh; don't rely on a single account.
  7. Record post-event status: how the affected person is doing now
  8. (returned to play, sent home, went to urgent care/ER, refused
  9. further care), not just what happened in the moment.
  10. Attach any relevant photos (of the scene, of visible injury if
  11. appropriate and consented to, of property damage) to support the
  12. record.
  13. Complete every section of the full incident report form —
  14. witnesses, statements, photos, post-event status, and anything
  15. from the in-the-moment response you need to correct or add detail
  16. to.
  17. Handle the report discreetly throughout — it names a minor. Store
  18. it only in the platform record; don't forward statements or photos
  19. over text or email, and don't discuss specifics with anyone
  20. outside the people who need to know to do their job.
  21. Consult the director before finalizing anything serious enough to
  22. carry insurance or legal exposure (a trip to the ER, a serious
  23. altercation, anything with a clear liability question) — this is
  24. the record the office and insurer treat as authoritative, so get
  25. it right rather than fast on anything consequential.
  26. Submit the finalized report. Nothing about this incident closes
  27. until this report is signed off — the incident follow-up activity
  28. depends on it being complete.
  29. If you're unreachable, the event lead drafts the report from the
  30. same witness statements and evidence, and the director finalizes
  31. it before it's considered closed.

Facility close walkthrough

You own this

When: After the last match concludes and spectators have cleared

There's a checklist for this — see the checklist slides.

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, escalate to Director per the standard handoff ladder; closure cannot be completed without a walkthrough sign-off.

  1. After the last match concludes and spectators have cleared, walk the
  2. same fixed route as the opening walkthrough: playing surfaces,
  3. locker rooms, restrooms, bleachers, then the parking lot.
  4. Check every locker room and restroom stall for anyone still inside;
  5. call out before entering an occupied-looking space.
  6. Confirm no personal belongings, equipment, or trash was left behind
  7. on the surfaces or in the stands; route anything found to lost and
  8. found.
  9. Confirm the day's equipment has been accounted for by facilities
  10. (equipment storage should already be underway).
  11. Check for any new safety issue from the day's use — damaged
  12. fixtures, spills, tripping hazards — and note it for the next
  13. opening walkthrough or an immediate work order.
  14. Confirm exterior areas (parking lot, entrances) are clear of people
  15. and hazards.
  16. Sign off the walkthrough — facility lock and alarm cannot start
  17. without this sign-off.

There's a checklist for this — see the checklist slides.

  1. Confirm the close walkthrough is signed off before starting —
  2. lock-up never happens first.
  3. Power down field/court lighting, scoreboards, and any powered
  4. equipment not needed overnight.
  5. Set HVAC to its overnight/unoccupied setting.
  6. Walk back through and lock all interior doors: locker rooms,
  7. storage/equipment room, concessions, office.
  8. Confirm interior lights are off except any required security
  9. lighting.
  10. Lock all exterior doors, confirming each is fully secured (not just
  11. latched).
  12. Set the alarm with the current code and wait for the confirmation
  13. tone or light before you leave — don't assume it armed.
  14. Confirm the perimeter is secure (gates latched, no propped doors) on
  15. your way to your vehicle.
  16. Log the lock-and-alarm completion. Alarm code rotation follows the
  17. centrally-enforced policy — never share or write down the code.

Staff clock-out

You own this

When: As each staff member finishes their last task

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, Event Lead witnesses the clock-out and escalates to Director per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. When a staff member finishes their last task for the day (final
  2. equipment stored, facility close walkthrough done, cash reconciled
  3. — whatever their role's closing task is), they come to the
  4. clock-out station before leaving the venue.
  5. Confirm their name against today's staffing plan and capture the
  6. actual end time — not the scheduled end time, the real one.
  7. Ask if there's anything to note for payroll or the office: worked
  8. past the scheduled end time, left early with venue manager
  9. sign-off, covered an extra task outside their normal role.
  10. The staff member signs to confirm the end time and notes are
  11. accurate.
  12. Front of house checks the signature against the staffing plan and
  13. signs to close that person's labor record for the day.
  14. Repeat for each staff member as they finish, in any order — this
  15. isn't a single end-of-day batch, it's per-person as people wrap up.
  16. Once every staffed slot for the day has a signed clock-out, the
  17. record is complete and ready to feed the payroll integration.
  18. If the venue manager isn't available to witness a clock-out, the
  19. event lead witnesses it and escalates per the escalation path.

Staff debrief

You own this

When: After facility close walkthrough is signed off

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, Event Lead runs the debrief and escalates to Director per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. Once the facility close walkthrough is signed off, gather whoever's
  2. still on shift for a 5-10 minute huddle — don't hold people who've
  3. already clocked out waiting for this.
  4. Ask what worked today: anything that ran smoother than expected,
  5. any process worth repeating.
  6. Ask what broke or ran rough: equipment failures, staffing gaps,
  7. confused parents, anything that slowed the day down.
  8. Ask if anything needs to go to the office: a facility repair
  9. request, a supply reorder, a schedule conflict for next time, a
  10. personnel note.
  11. Capture incident or safety mentions even briefly — the full
  12. incident report is a separate activity, but the debrief should
  13. flag that one happened so it isn't lost.
  14. Write the notes into the debrief form in plain language — this
  15. feeds directly into tonight's daily digest and the weekly review,
  16. so vague notes ("things were fine") aren't useful to future you.
  17. Submit the form before leaving the venue.
  18. If the venue manager isn't available, the event lead runs the
  19. debrief and escalates per the escalation path.

Incident follow-up

You own this

When: 24 hours to 48 hours after incident report finalization

If something goes wrong: If Venue Manager unreachable, Event Lead conducts follow-up outreach and escalates to Director per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. Within 24-48 hours of the finalized incident report, follow up
  2. directly with the affected family (or staff member, if it's a
  3. staff incident) — a phone call or in-person check-in, not just a
  4. text.
  5. Ask about recovery status and, for a player, whether or when they
  6. expect to return to play — record what they tell you, not what
  7. you assume.
  8. If the incident involves any injury claim, confirm whether the
  9. family has been in contact with insurance and capture the contact
  10. status (not yet, in progress, resolved).
  11. Note whether the family has any outstanding question or concern
  12. from the incident that the office should address — don't let this
  13. follow-up be a formality if they still have something unresolved.
  14. Decide whether any additional action is needed from the office: a
  15. policy referral (feeds the weekly safety review), a facility
  16. referral (feeds the field damage report if one hasn't already been
  17. filed), or a direct follow-up from the director.
  18. Record the outreach, what you learned, and any recommended
  19. additional action on the incident follow-up form, and submit it.
  20. Handle this discreetly like every other step in the incident chain
  21. — it still names a minor; keep the conversation and the record
  22. between you, the family, and anyone else who needs to know.
  23. If you're unreachable within the window, the event lead conducts
  24. the outreach and escalates to the director per the standard
  25. handoff ladder — the 24-48h window doesn't stretch just because
  26. you're unavailable.
