  3. venue's safety standard — an unsecured goal is a serious tip-over
  4. hazard).
  5. Place corner flags and any required field markings for the sport.
  6. Place team benches on the correct sidelines per the match
  7. assignment.

Flag football field line check

You own this

When: About 30 minutes before each flag football kickoff

If something goes wrong: If Facilities unreachable, escalate to Venue Manager per the standard handoff ladder.

  1. About 30 minutes before each flag football kickoff, walk the full
  2. length of the field.
  3. Verify yard lines are visible and accurately spaced per the field's
  4. marking standard.
  5. Verify both end zones are clearly marked at the correct depth.
  6. Verify sideline and out-of-bounds markings are visible along the
  7. full length of the field.
  8. Check flag-pulling zone markings if the format uses them, and
  9. confirm no line has faded or shifted since the last match.
  10. Touch up or re-mark anything faded or unclear before the match
  11. starts — don't let players guess at boundaries.
  12. Photograph the field lines and upload the photo to the match record.
  13. If field conditions (mud, standing water) are obscuring lines faster
  14. than they can be maintained, flag it to the venue manager — this may
  15. factor into a rainout call.

  1. At the final whistle, move immediately to the just-finished surface
  2. — the next match's crew is on the clock behind you.
  3. Collect match-specific equipment: balls, pinnies, corner flags, and
  4. anything not staying with the field.
  5. Re-secure goals/nets if they were repositioned during the match;

  12. anything missing.

Field damage report

You own this

When: Damage observed at any point during or after a match

If something goes wrong: If Facilities unreachable, Venue Manager logs the damage and escalates to Director for repair authorization.

  1. When you notice or are told about damage to a playing surface or
  2. facility — turf burns, goal damage, broken equipment, vandalism —
  3. go assess it in person before filing anything; don't file off a
  4. secondhand description alone if you can get eyes on it in a
  5. reasonable time.
  6. Pull the most recent field condition photo (pregame) for that
  7. surface if one's on file — it's your baseline for showing what's
  8. new versus pre-existing wear.
  9. Take dated photos of the damage from a distance (showing location
  10. and context) and up close (showing the specific damage), and note
  11. whether the surface or area is safe to keep using or needs to come
  12. out of rotation right away.
  13. If the damage makes the surface unsafe for continued play, flag
  14. the venue manager immediately to pull it from the day's schedule —
  15. don't wait for the report to be filed before raising that.
  16. Note when and how the damage likely occurred if it's evident
  17. (during a specific match, overnight, weather-related, vandalism) —
  18. this matters for any charge-back decision.
  19. Estimate repair urgency (can wait for routine maintenance vs. needs
  20. prompt repair) and, if you can, a rough sense of scope (spot repair
  21. vs. bigger job) — you're not pricing it, just giving the office
  22. enough to prioritize.
  23. Complete the field damage report form with the photos, baseline
  24. comparison, cause if known, and urgency, and submit it.
  25. Consult the venue manager on anything ambiguous (is this normal
  26. wear or real damage, does this need to come out of rotation)
  27. before finalizing, and escalate to the director for repair
  28. authorization on anything beyond routine maintenance or any damage
  29. that might be charged back to a team or renter — don't authorize a
  30. chargeback call yourself.
  31. If you're unreachable, the venue manager logs the damage and
  32. escalates to the director for repair authorization per the
  33. escalation path.
