# Safety & Policy Standards Reference

> Source basis for the operational SOPs. Compiled 2026-07-06 from a multi-source, adversarially-verified research pass (NATA, Korey Stringer Institute, NWS, CPSC/ASTM, Ohio Revised Code, CDC, and published youth-league policies). This is a research synthesis to ground procedures — **not legal advice**. Confirm the concussion and abuse-prevention items with counsel before finalizing policy.

The SOPs in `docs/operations/catalog/` should cite these numbers rather than invented ones. Where a threshold is adopted, the citation goes in the activity's `sop_body`.

## 1. Heat — use WBGT, not heat index

**Adopt:** Environmental heat modification is based on **Wet Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT)**, not the heat index or a single fixed air temperature. Monitor with a WBGT device (3–4 ft off the ground, in the sun, ≥15 min exposure) **every 30 minutes whenever it is over 70 °F** at the venue.

**Graduated response (US Soccer / KSI model, adoptable):**
- Modify play (extra water breaks, shorter periods, no doubleheaders) starting around **WBGT 85.8 °F**, with tighter limits at **87.8 °F**.
- **Postpone / limit / cancel at WBGT ≥ 89.8 °F.**
- KSI flag-color rest breaks if you run structured sessions: green ≥3×3 min/hr; yellow 3×4 min; orange 4×4 min + 2-hr cap; red 1-hr cap + 20 min rest.

**On-site emergency:** Suspected exertional heat stroke (core temp >104–105 °F + confusion) → **"cool first, transport second"**: cold-water immersion (35–59 °F), cool to ~102 °F before EMS transport. Rectal temperature is the only valid field measurement.

**Sources:** NATA 2015 Exertional Heat Illnesses Position Statement; Korey Stringer Institute WBGT monitoring guidance; US Soccer PDI heat thresholds.
**Change from current SOP:** the "heat index reaches 95 °F" line is replaced by the WBGT model above (outdoor activities only).

## 2. Lightning — the 30-30 rule

**Adopt:** Stop play at the **first thunder or lightning**. Evacuate to safe shelter when a storm is within **5 nautical miles (~6 miles)**. Resume only after **30 minutes with no thunder and no lightning** — the 30-minute clock **restarts on every** strike or thunderclap.

- A **designated weather monitor** watches conditions — explicitly **not** the coach, referee, or official (they can't monitor while working).
- Safe shelter = a substantial enclosed building (wiring + plumbing) or a fully enclosed hard-top metal vehicle. **Dugouts, tents, gazebos, and park/picnic shelters are not safe shelter.**
- A written lightning plan should state when to stop, where to shelter, when to resume, who monitors, and what to do if someone is struck.

**Sources:** NATA Lightning Safety for Athletics & Recreation; NWS lightning safety guidance.
**Change from current SOP:** the 30-second flash-to-bang + 30-minute all-clear draft is correct and now attributed; adds the 5-nmi evacuation trigger, the separate-monitor requirement, and the shelter definition.

## 3. Rain / field playability — not a rain percentage

**Finding:** Well-run youth and adult clubs' published weather policies contain **no rain-percentage criterion**. Go/no-go is driven by (a) **lightning** — the only critical stop factor — and (b) **field playability** judged on site (standing water, footing/firmness, safety of the surface), plus WBGT/air-quality where relevant.

**Adopt:** Replace "chance of rain ≥ 60 %" with a **field-playability assessment**: is the surface safe and playable (no standing water, stable footing)? Rain alone doesn't stop play; unsafe field conditions or lightning do.

**Sources:** NWS ("lightning, not rainfall, is the critical factor"); published field-closure policies of large youth clubs (Loudoun Soccer and peers) which omit rain-% thresholds.
**Change from current SOP:** the pre-day "60 % chance of rain" flag becomes a field-condition + lightning model, matching the owner's read that it's field-condition based.

## 4. Movable goal anchoring

**Adopt:** **Every movable goal is anchored or counterweighted at all times** — this is the core CPSC/ASTM safety requirement (ASTM F2056 specifies **200 lb tip-over resistance**). Concrete parameters:
- **Auger/screw anchors:** minimum **two per goal** (one each side), more per manufacturer spec / soil.
- **Peg/stake anchors:** **two to four per goal** (more for heavier goals), ~**10-inch** stakes driven at an angle.
- **Indoor / artificial turf** (anchors can't penetrate): **sandbags or counterweights sized to the goal's total weight** (goals typically 150–500 lb).
- **Never** use net pegs to anchor the goal itself.
- **Inspection:** coach inspects goals **before activities**; referee inspects **before every game** (Montana Youth Soccer 2-1100 model).
- **Unused goals:** locked in storage or chained to a fixed structure; nets removed when not in use; **children never move or hang on goals**; use only on level fields; visible safety labels on crossbar and posts.

**Sources:** CPSC "Guidelines for Movable Soccer Goals"; ASTM F2056; Montana Youth Soccer policy 2-1100.
**Change from current SOP:** the "anchored or weighted per the venue's safety standard" placeholder is replaced by these specifics; adds the coach-before / ref-before-every-game inspection to the relevant activities.

## 5. Concussion — Ohio law binds this operator (youth)

**Key finding — this is a legal obligation, not best practice.** Ohio's youth concussion / Return-to-Play law **explicitly covers "youth sports organizations"** (any public or nonpublic entity organizing athletics for athletes ≤19), **not just schools**. A private/rec youth league in Ohio is bound by it. Codified at **ORC 3707.511** (youth sports organizations), **3313.539** (schools, parallel), and **3707.52** (information sheet); effective April 26, 2013.

**Required of a youth operator:**
1. **Remove from play:** any athlete showing concussion signs is removed immediately by a coach, referee, or official and **may not return the same day**.
2. **Written clearance to return:** the athlete may return only after evaluation and **written clearance by a physician or Ohio-authorized licensed health-care provider**.
3. **Training:** coaches and referees must hold **concussion-recognition training** (CDC HEADS UP or NFHS, state-linked) completed **within the past three years**, or a Pupil Activity Permit.
4. **Annual information sheet:** distribute the **Ohio Department of Health Youth Sports Concussion Information Sheet** to athletes and parents/guardians **annually at registration** (rec leagues not registering through OSA must do this themselves).
5. Compliant organizations have **civil immunity** except for willful/wanton misconduct.

**Adults:** no statutory concussion mandate for adult play, but the remove-from-play + medical-clearance model is sound safety practice and cheap to apply uniformly.

**Sources:** ORC 3313.539 / 3707.511 / 3707.52; CDC HEADS UP.
**Change from current SOP:** the injury/notification procedure gains the mandatory remove-no-same-day-return + written-clearance steps; the concussion training requirement ties into the existing coach-credentials system; registration must surface the ODH info sheet annually.

## 6. Abuse prevention & background checks

**SafeSport Act — genuinely ambiguous for a purely-local league.** Its clear statutory reach is National Governing Bodies and amateur orgs under U.S. Olympic Committee jurisdiction. Broader readings extend it to local youth orgs **if** they participate in interstate/international competition **or** include adults who regularly interact with minor athletes. So it may or may not legally bind Aspire today, and **would** bind it the moment it runs interstate/travel play.

**Adopt as best practice regardless** (this is the child-safety standard and supports "keep bad actors out"):
- **Mandatory reporting:** covered adults report any suspicion of child sexual abuse to law enforcement **within 24 hours**.
- **Limit one-on-one adult–minor interactions** unless another adult is present/observable.
- **Abuse-prevention training** covering grooming and boundary violations.
- **Written procedures** for reporting suspected abuse and handling allegations.

**Background checks — Ohio does NOT statutorily mandate them for youth sports volunteers** (only ~13 states do; Ohio is not one). So Aspire's background-check requirement is **self-imposed best practice**. Adoptable baseline (Little League model): **annual** re-screening of everyone providing regular service (coaches, staff, board), including **criminal-history + sex-offender-registry** checks, with periodic renewal.

**Sources:** Protecting Young Victims from Sexual Abuse / SafeSport Authorization Act; Little League screening policy; 50-state background-check survey (Dec 2025).
**Change from current SOP:** ties the ejection/suspension tracker and coach-credentials work to a written abuse-prevention + reporting policy; confirms background checks are policy-driven (already implemented in the compliance system), not statute-driven.

## 7. Weather cancellation — refunds vs. credit

**Finding — the owner's "no weather refunds, reschedule → credit" is industry standard.**
- Large clubs (e.g., Loudoun Soccer) issue **no refunds** for weather-cancelled games and rely on **rescheduling**.
- Tournament operators (e.g., SMC Soccer) issue **tiered credits by games played**: **75 % credit if no games played, 50 % after one, 25 % after two**; optional cancellation insurance (~10 % of entry) upgrades to a full/prorated refund.
- Common framing: not every scheduled session is guaranteed; rescheduling is the default; administrative fees are non-refundable.

**Adopt:** no cash refunds for weather; **reschedule first, then account credit**; cash refunds only for company-caused cancellations, director-approved. For multi-session programs, the tiered-credit-by-sessions-delivered model is a clean, defensible rule.

**Sources:** published refund policies of Loudoun Soccer and SMC Soccer (and peers).
**Change from current SOP:** confirms and specifies the refund/credit policy the owner set; the tiered-credit option is available for programs that run multiple guaranteed sessions.
