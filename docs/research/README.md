# Aspire Sports: Evidence-Based Development Research

This directory contains research briefs synthesizing global best practices for youth athlete development. The goal is to embed these principles directly into the Aspire Sports platform.

---

## Executive Summary

**The Problem:** US youth sports optimizes for the wrong things—early wins, revenue, travel teams—while neglecting what actually matters: long-term athlete development.

**The Evidence:** Research from Europe (particularly Scandinavia), alongside emerging US initiatives, shows that development-focused environments produce better athletes AND happier kids who stay in sports longer.

**The Opportunity:** No current sports management platform embeds development principles into its core design. Aspire Sports can be the first.

## Core Principles

### From the Research

1. **Environment > Curriculum** — The context matters more than the content
2. **Long-term > Short-term** — Process over results, especially for young athletes
3. **Holistic > Athletic-only** — Personal development alongside sports development
4. **Individual > System** — Adapt to the athlete, not athlete to system
5. **Multi-sport > Specialization** — Especially before age 12
6. **Coach quality > Facilities** — A great coach in a bad field beats the reverse

### For the Software

1. **Embed, don't separate** — Development guidance at point of need
2. **Measure what matters** — Development metrics, not just admin metrics
3. **Protect against harm** — Defaults and warnings prevent bad practices
4. **Align stakeholders** — Parents, coaches, admins see same priorities
5. **Long-term view** — Athletes span years, not just seasons
6. **Make development visible** — Celebrate progress, not just wins

## Research Gaps Identified

Areas needing more investigation:
- Specific curriculum content for each sport/age
- Technology adoption patterns among volunteer coaches
- Parent education effectiveness
- Equity and accessibility in development-focused models
- Longitudinal outcomes of development-focused programs

## Next Steps

1. **Validate with practitioners** — Interview coaches, administrators, parents
2. **Prioritize features** — Based on impact and feasibility
3. **Design prototypes** — Development-focused interfaces
4. **Build incrementally** — Start with highest-impact features
5. **Measure outcomes** — Track whether software changes behavior
