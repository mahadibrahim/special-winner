# Research Brief #2: Age-Appropriate Training Guidelines

**Key Finding:** What works at age 14 harms at age 8. Development must match developmental stage.

---

## The Golden Rule

> **Children should not train more hours per week than their age in years.**
> — American Academy of Pediatrics

An 8-year-old: max 8 hours/week of organized sports.
A 12-year-old: max 12 hours/week of organized sports.

---

## Stage-by-Stage Guidelines

### Ages 3-5: Discovery Stage

**Goal:** Fall in love with movement

| Aspect | Guideline |
|--------|-----------|
| Session length | 30-45 minutes max |
| Frequency | 1-2x per week |
| Format | Play-based, no formal instruction |
| Competition | None |
| Focus | Running, jumping, throwing, catching, tumbling |

**What to do:**
- Tag games, obstacle courses, free play with balls
- Let kids explore without correction
- Keep activities varied (attention span: 5-10 minutes per activity)

**What NOT to do:**
- Formal drills or lines
- Score-keeping or standings
- Position assignments
- Criticism of technique

---

### Ages 6-8: Fundamentals Stage

**Goal:** Build movement vocabulary through variety

| Aspect | Guideline |
|--------|-----------|
| Session length | 45-60 minutes |
| Frequency | 2-3x per week (across different sports) |
| Practice:Game ratio | 3:1 |
| Competition | Minimal, equal playing time required |
| Focus | Basic sport skills + continued general movement |

**Skill focus by sport (soccer example):**
- 60% dribbling and ball mastery
- 20% basic passing (push pass)
- 10% shooting (inside of foot)
- 10% fun games and scrimmages

**What to do:**
- Maximum touches on ball (avoid lines)
- Pair work (this age works best in twos)
- Guided discovery ("What happens if you use the outside of your foot?")
- Multiple sports strongly encouraged

**What NOT to do:**
- Complex tactics or positions
- Heading (not safe until U11+)
- Tryouts or team cuts
- Specialization in one sport
- Yelling corrections from sideline

---

### Ages 9-10: Skill Building Stage

**Goal:** Refine technique through repetition

| Aspect | Guideline |
|--------|-----------|
| Session length | 60-75 minutes |
| Frequency | 3-4x per week (still multi-sport) |
| Practice:Game ratio | 2:1 |
| Competition | Moderate, still emphasize development |
| Focus | Sport-specific technique + introduce tactics |

**Skill focus (soccer example):**
- 40% ball mastery and dribbling moves
- 25% passing and receiving
- 20% shooting and finishing
- 15% small-sided games (4v4 to 7v7)

**Training structure:**
1. Warm-up with ball (10 min)
2. Technical work in pairs/small groups (20 min)
3. Small-sided games with constraints (20 min)
4. Scrimmage (15 min)
5. Cool-down (5 min)

**What to do:**
- Introduce bodyweight strength training
- Teach basic tactical concepts through games, not lectures
- Allow players to make decisions and mistakes
- 2-3 sports still recommended

**What NOT to do:**
- Position specialization (everyone plays everywhere)
- Extended tactical lectures
- Conditioning separate from ball work
- Cutting players from teams

---

### Ages 11-12: Development Stage

**Goal:** Apply skills under pressure

| Aspect | Guideline |
|--------|-----------|
| Session length | 75-90 minutes |
| Frequency | 3-4x per week |
| Practice:Game ratio | 1.5:1 |
| Competition | Standings matter more, but development still primary |
| Focus | Skill refinement + tactical understanding |

**Key changes:**
- Players entering puberty at different rates - be patient with awkward phases
- Can introduce position preferences (not locked positions)
- Group tactical concepts become relevant
- Basic periodization can begin

**What to do:**
- Video review of their own play
- Introduce sport psychology basics (goal-setting, visualization)
- Allow more competitive intensity in training
- Maintain at least one other sport or activity

**What NOT to do:**
- Assume physical maturity = readiness for adult training
- Over-emphasize early vs late developers
- Abandon technique work for tactics-only
- Year-round single-sport commitment

---

### Ages 13-15: Competitive Stage

**Goal:** Performance development with continued growth

| Aspect | Guideline |
|--------|-----------|
| Session length | 90-120 minutes |
| Frequency | 4-5x per week |
| Competition | Results matter, but long-term still priority |
| Focus | Position-specific skills + team tactics + physical development |

**New elements:**
- Supervised strength training (proper form critical during growth spurts)
- Position-specific training
- Mental skills training
- Nutrition education
- Injury prevention focus

**Critical consideration:** Growth spurts create injury risk. Monitor for:
- Osgood-Schlatter (knee pain)
- Sever's disease (heel pain)
- Reduced coordination during rapid growth

**Off-season requirement:** 1-2 complementary activities; full rest periods

---

### Ages 16-18: Refinement Stage

**Goal:** Prepare for adult competition or transition out

| Aspect | Guideline |
|--------|-----------|
| Session length | 2-3 hours |
| Frequency | 5-6x per week for serious athletes |
| Focus | Periodized training, peak performance, career planning |

**Key additions:**
- Formal periodization (preparation, competition, recovery phases)
- Advanced video analysis
- College recruitment support (if applicable)
- Leadership and mentoring roles
- Honest conversations about realistic pathways

---

## Multi-Sport Requirement

Research consistently shows:

| Finding | Source |
|---------|--------|
| Elite adult athletes played multiple sports as youth | Côté (1999) |
| Early specializers have higher burnout/dropout rates | Multiple studies |
| Multi-sport athletes have fewer overuse injuries | AAP |
| Late specializers outperform early specializers long-term | Swedish longitudinal study |

**Recommendation by age:**
- Under 10: 3+ sports required
- Ages 10-12: 2-3 sports recommended
- Ages 13-15: Primary sport + 1 complementary activity
- Ages 16+: Specialization acceptable with proper off-season

---

## Red Flags by Age

### Ages 6-10
- Playing same sport year-round
- More than 2 games per weekend
- Tryouts that cut players
- Travel more than 1 hour for games
- Adults yelling during games

### Ages 11-14
- Training more hours than age in years
- Persistent pain dismissed as "toughness"
- Declining enjoyment/motivation
- Social isolation from non-sport peers
- Parent pressure about scholarships

### Ages 15+
- No off-season rest (minimum 4-6 weeks)
- Ignoring growth-related injuries
- No plan B beyond athletics
- Overtraining symptoms (fatigue, illness, declining performance)

---

## Software Implications

A development-focused platform should:

1. **Enforce age-appropriate defaults**
   - Session length limits by age
   - Practice:game ratio recommendations
   - Multi-sport tracking and encouragement

2. **Provide stage-specific curricula**
   - Different skill focuses by age
   - Age-appropriate drills and activities
   - Progressive complexity

3. **Alert on red flags**
   - Too many hours per week
   - Year-round single sport
   - Tournament overload

4. **Track development, not just results**
   - Skills progression over time
   - Effort and improvement metrics
   - Enjoyment/motivation check-ins
