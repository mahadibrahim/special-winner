# Research Brief #3: Effective Coaching Practices

**Key Finding:** How coaches behave matters more than what they know. Trained coaches retain 95% of players; untrained coaches lose 26%.

---

## The Problem

Current state of youth coaching in the US:

| Statistic | Implication |
|-----------|-------------|
| 6.5 million youth coaches | Massive scale |
| < 20% trained in motivational techniques | Most coaches don't know how to communicate with kids |
| Only 33% trained in sport skills/tactics | Most coaches don't know the sport well enough |
| 5% quit rate (trained coaches) | Training works |
| 26% quit rate (untrained coaches) | Untrained coaches drive kids away |

---

## The Three Essential Training Areas

Every youth coach needs training in:

### 1. Coaching Philosophy (How to Work with Kids)

**The Double-Goal Approach** (Positive Coaching Alliance):
- Goal 1: Winning (matters, but secondary)
- Goal 2: Teaching life lessons (primary)

**ELM Framework for Feedback:**
- **E**ffort - Praise effort, not talent
- **L**earning - Frame everything as learning opportunity
- **M**istakes are OK - Normalize failure as part of growth

**Practical behaviors:**
| Instead of... | Do this... |
|---------------|------------|
| "You missed!" | "Good effort. What did you see?" |
| "Pay attention!" | "Eyes here for 10 seconds" |
| "That's wrong" | "Try it this way" |
| Yelling across field | Walk over, speak calmly |
| Lecturing for 5 minutes | Demonstrate in 30 seconds |

### 2. Physical Literacy & Sport Skills

Coaches must understand:
- Age-appropriate activities (see Research Brief #2)
- Proper technique for fundamental skills
- How to teach progressively (simple → complex)
- How to maximize touches/repetitions

**The critical ratio:**
- Kids should be ACTIVE 70%+ of practice time
- Research shows ~30% of practice time is wasted on kids standing and listening

### 3. Basic Safety

Minimum requirements:
- CPR certification
- Concussion recognition and protocol
- Heat illness prevention
- Basic first aid
- SafeSport training (abuse prevention)

## Practice Design That Works

### Session Structure (Research-Backed)

**Optimal flow:**
```
Warm-up with ball (10-15%)
    ↓
Technical work - pairs/small groups (25-30%)
    ↓
Small-sided games with constraints (30-35%)
    ↓
Scrimmage/game application (20-25%)
    ↓
Cool-down and recap (5%)
```

**Key principle:** Progress from "training form" (drills) to "playing form" (games) within each session.

### Coaching Behaviors During Practice

**What works:**

| Behavior | Why it works |
|----------|--------------|
| **Questioning** | "What could you do differently?" promotes thinking |
| **Divergent questions** | Open-ended questions develop problem-solving |
| **Corrective feedback** | Specific, actionable feedback (not just "good job") |
| **Demonstrations** | Show, don't just tell |
| **Constraints manipulation** | Change rules/space to create learning challenges |

**What doesn't work:**

| Behavior | Why it fails |
|----------|--------------|
| **Excessive instruction** | Creates dependency, kills creativity |
| **Stopping play constantly** | Reduces learning opportunities |
| **Generic praise** | "Good job" has no learning value |
| **Lecturing** | Kids tune out after 30 seconds |
| **Concurrent instruction** | Talking while they're trying to execute |

### The 3-Second Rule

When a player makes a mistake:
1. **Wait 3 seconds** before intervening
2. See if they self-correct
3. If not, ask a question: "What did you notice?"
4. Only then offer specific guidance

## The Constraints-Led Approach

Instead of prescriptive coaching ("Do it this way"), manipulate the environment:

**Task constraints:** Rules, objectives, equipment
- "You can only score with your weak foot"
- "Ball must touch 3 players before shooting"
- Smaller goals, different ball sizes

**Environmental constraints:** Space, surface, obstacles
- Smaller field = more touches
- Larger field = more running/decision-making
- Uneven numbers (3v2) = overload situations

**Player constraints:** Team composition
- Mixed abilities together
- Older with younger occasionally
- Different positions than usual

**Why this works:** Players discover solutions rather than following instructions. This builds adaptable, creative athletes.

## Communication Scripts

### Before Practice

```
"Today we're working on [skill].
By the end, you'll be able to [specific outcome].
Remember: mistakes help us learn. Try new things."
```

### During Practice

| Situation | Script |
|-----------|--------|
| Player succeeds | "I saw you [specific action]. That's exactly what helps us [outcome]." |
| Player struggles | "That's a tough one. What's one thing you could try differently?" |
| Player frustrated | "Take a breath. Everyone struggles with this at first. You'll get it." |
| Player unfocused | Walk over, make eye contact, "I need your eyes for 10 seconds." |

### After Practice

```
"Today you worked hard on [skill].
I noticed [specific positive observation].
This week, practice [one thing] at home.
Great effort today."
```

### To Parents (Weekly)

```
"This week we focused on [skill].
Your child showed [specific positive].
At home, you can help by [one simple thing].
Please avoid coaching from the sideline - it confuses them."
```

## The Feedback Ratio

Research-backed guideline: **5:1 positive to corrective ratio**

For every correction, provide 5 positive observations.

**Why:** Young athletes' self-efficacy is heavily influenced by coach feedback. Excessive criticism damages confidence and motivation.

**Practical application:**
1. Catch them doing something right - comment on it
2. When correcting, sandwich: positive → correction → encouragement
3. End every interaction positively

## Training Delivery That Works

Research shows coaches learn best when:

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Acknowledge experience** | Build on what they already know |
| **Propose, don't impose** | Offer options, let coaches adapt |
| **Practical focus** | Field experience > classroom lecture |
| **Immediate application** | Practice new skills same week |
| **Ongoing support** | Not one-time workshop; continued coaching |

**Effective formats:**
- 4-hour workshops mixing theory + practice
- On-field mentoring with experienced coach
- Video review of own coaching
- Peer coaching circles
- Just-in-time resources (mobile app at practice)

## Red Flags: Coaching Behaviors to Eliminate

- [ ] Yelling criticism during games
- [ ] Running laps as punishment
- [ ] Benching players for mistakes
- [ ] Lectures longer than 2 minutes
- [ ] Lines where kids wait (more than 10 seconds)
- [ ] "My way or the highway" approach
- [ ] Comparing players to each other
- [ ] Discussing playing time with parents during games
- [ ] Ignoring weaker players
- [ ] Focusing only on "best" players

## Software Implications

A development-focused platform should:

1. **Provide practice plans**
   - Age-appropriate, pre-built sessions
   - Proper warm-up → skills → games progression
   - Time allocations built in

2. **Guide coaching behavior**
   - Communication scripts for common situations
   - Feedback prompts during/after sessions
   - Red flag warnings for problematic patterns

3. **Support coach development**
   - Track certification status
   - Just-in-time tips relevant to current session
   - Video examples of techniques

4. **Enable feedback loops**
   - Player enjoyment surveys
   - Parent feedback mechanism
   - Self-assessment tools for coaches

5. **Deliver training in context**
   - Not separate certification portals
   - Embedded in the workflow
   - Short, actionable, when needed
