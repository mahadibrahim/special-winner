# Research Brief — 2026-07-05

First live `/curriculum-refinery research` run. Four parallel sweeps:
international federations & academies, US federations, LTAD/athlete-development
literature, YouTube coaching content (adapted, never transcribed). Findings
curated to what informs the wave-2 build-out (hockey delivery content, baseball
fundamentals, PH/PS above age 8, competitive stage 13–15). Every claim carries
a citation; recency noted where it matters.

## Headline findings

### 1. Hockey activity/session design (feeds: hockey activities + session plans)

- USA Hockey ADM: 8U = station-based practices + cross-ice 4v4 games with
  intermediate nets; 6U = 3v3 with mini nets, no goalies. Stations 8–10 min
  each (other ADM materials: 30–60s demo + 4–7 min work per station);
  practice mix ≈ **70% individual skills / 20% small-area games / 10% team
  tactics**. (usahockey.com/news_article/show/1072320;
  portal.usahockey.com …/basic_structure_for_8u.pdf;
  joinstriveon.com/blog/hockey-practice-plan)
- Hockey Canada U11: 85% skills+tactics / 15% team play; weekly structure =
  1 skills session + 1 small-area-games session + 1 game.
  (hockeycanada.ca …/under-11/associations/seasonal-structure)
- IIHF-hosted Sweden/Finland small-area-games study: up to 5× more shot
  attempts in small-ice games vs full-ice 5v5; Sweden bans checking until 14.
  (blob.iihf.com …/sag-study-fihaandsiha_growingthegame_2020_6_17.pdf)
- Adapted game concepts with credible sources for writers (USA Hockey ADM,
  Ice Hockey Systems, Hockey Canada Drill Hub, Weiss Tech): Sharks & Minnows,
  Clean Your Room, Toy Finder, Corner Tires, 1v1v1 mini nets, King/Queen of
  the Hill, Musical Pucks, Border Tag (ADM's own 8U pick). Full list with
  URLs in the sweep output; note our elimination-free default policy applies
  when adapting Musical Pucks/Knockout-family games.

### 2. Baseball fundamentals (feeds: baseball skills build-out)

- USA Baseball LTAD ages 6–8: core motor competencies (run, throw, catch,
  strike/hit — an unordered set; our teaching sequence is an editorial
  choice), taught through play; multi-sport explicitly championed;
  station-based practice recommended. (usabaseball.com LTAD; summarized at
  joeeisenmann.substack.com/p/revisiting-usa-baseballs-long-term)
- Keep rules/strategy content minimal at 6–8: which base to run to, where to
  stand, what an out is — the rest is noise. (gorout.com/youth-baseball-training)
- Pitch Smart daily limits (for later stages; cite when pitching content
  arrives): 75/day at 9–10, 85/day at 11–12, tiered rest, ≥4 months/year off
  throwing. Japan's own youth limits now converge on similar numbers (Pony
  85; 15U 70/day) after walking back its volume culture.
  (mlb.com/pitch-smart; baseballjapan.org)
- Adapted activity concepts with sources (Little League University,
  PlaySportsTV ages-5-8 series, Dominate The Diamond, Dan Blewett): Defend
  the Castle (fielding), grip→step→throw progression, two-hand catching
  ("fingers up above the belt"), catch-transfer separation, Run the Bases
  cue games.

### 3. Physical + psychological, ages 9–15 (feeds: PH/PS above fundamentals + competitive stage)

- **Represent trainability windows honestly**: the classic Balyi
  "windows of trainability" lack empirical consensus (Sports Health 2021
  narrative review, PMC8669922); maturation status predicts trainability
  better than chronological age (agility scoping review, Frontiers 2022).
  Author PH content as maturation-anchored heuristics, not fixed age locks.
- Pre-PHV (≈9–12): high-neural-demand work (sprints, plyo, coordination);
  post-PHV adds structural strength. NSCA position: resistance training safe
  from ~6–7 with instruction-following maturity — "stunts growth" is folk
  myth. (nsca.com position stands)
- PHV window (13–15): strength/power dip during the spurt ("adolescent
  awkwardness") but training through it blunts the decline — keep frequency,
  add coordination work, reassess maturity offset 2–3×/year (2025 scoping
  review, PMC12101259).
- Psychological skills: teach **process goals over outcome goals** and
  preparatory self-talk at 9–12 (PMC3834981); autonomy/competence/relatedness
  (BPN) is the protective mechanism against burnout — 13–15 PS content
  should build goal ownership, not imposed goals (2025 burnout scoping
  review, PMC11868109; Frontiers 2025 PMC12378512).
- Specialization guardrails for competitive-stage copy: delay single-sport
  specialization to ~13+ (AOSSM 2016 consensus, PMC4853833); **hours/week ≤
  age, ceiling 16**; ≥1–2 rest days/week, ≥3 months/year off a single sport
  (AAP 2016 + AAP 2024 overuse/burnout update, publications.aap.org
  …/e2023065129). NATA concurs. LTAD train-to-train ratio ≈ 60–75% training
  vs competition; Learn-to-Train (9–12) ≈ 70:30.
- RAE mitigation worth one coaching principle: bio-banding reduces (not
  eliminates) relative-age bias; birthday-banding is a documented
  alternative (Pediatric Exercise Science 2024; PMC7739587).
- England FA DNA Foundation Phase four-corner model (technical/physical/
  psychological/social, sub-banded 5–8 and 9–12) independently validates our
  four-domain structure and the audit's PH/PS gap. (thefa.com/bootroom)

### 4. Platform-level finding (NOT curriculum content — owner attention)

- **US Youth Soccer / AYSO / US Club Soccer move age-group formation from
  calendar-year to an Aug 1–Jul 31 school-year cycle, effective the 2026–27
  season** (announced June 2025).
  (usyouthsoccer.org/news/2025/06/10/updated-decision-on-age-group-formation;
  ayso.org/age-group-revision-2026-2027)
  If any program age-group logic, registration copy, or curriculum age-band
  text assumes birth-year cutoffs, it needs review before the 2026–27
  registration cycle. Flagged as a proposed directive AND a platform issue.
