## ARTICLE III — MANAGEMENT

**3.1 Manager-Managed.** The Company is manager-managed. [MANAGING MEMBER NAME] is designated as the "Managing Member" and shall serve until resignation, removal for Cause (Section 9.3), death, or Disability.

**3.2 Authority of Managing Member.** Except as limited by Section 3.3, the Managing Member has full authority to manage the business and affairs of the Company, including: executing contracts; hiring and firing employees and contractors; opening and managing bank accounts; setting pricing and operational strategy; filing tax returns; and binding the Company in the ordinary course of business.

**3.3 Major Decisions Requiring Supermajority Approval.** Notwithstanding Section 3.2, the following actions require the written approval of Members holding at least 75% of the Units (effectively requiring both Members to agree):

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) Sale, merger, or dissolution of the Company or sale of substantially all of its assets;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) Admission of new Members or issuance of new Units;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) Incurrence of debt exceeding $50,000 in aggregate outstanding;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) Capital expenditures exceeding $25,000 in a single transaction or $75,000 cumulatively per fiscal year;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(e) Amendment of this Agreement or the Articles of Organization;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(f) Change in the Company's tax classification;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(g) Adjustment of the Managing Member's base salary above the annual cap in Section 4.1(b);
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(h) Entering into any transaction with a Member or their affiliate (related-party transactions);
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(i) Distribution of any amount other than Tax Distributions under Section 5.2.

**3.4 Standard of Care.** The Managing Member shall act in good faith, in the best interests of the Company, and with the care an ordinarily prudent person in a like position would exercise under similar circumstances. The Managing Member shall owe the duties of loyalty and care set forth in ORC § 1706.31.

**3.5 Time Commitment.** The Managing Member shall devote substantially full business time and attention to the Company, subject to permitted outside activities disclosed in writing to the Non-Managing Member.

## ARTICLE IV — MANAGING MEMBER COMPENSATION

**4.1 Base Salary.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) The Managing Member shall receive an annual base salary ("Base Salary") for services rendered to the Company, payable in equal installments no less frequently than monthly. The Base Salary is treated as a guaranteed payment under IRC § 707(c) (or as W-2 wages if the Company elects S-corporation treatment) and is an expense of the Company paid **before** calculation of Net Profits available for distribution.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **Initial Base Salary schedule:**

| Period | Annual Base Salary |
|---|---|
| Year 1 (launch year, trailing revenue < $250,000) | $60,000 |
| Year 2 or trailing 12-mo revenue $250,000–$500,000 | $75,000 |
| Trailing 12-mo revenue $500,000–$1,000,000 | $90,000 |
| Trailing 12-mo revenue $1,000,000–$2,000,000 | $120,000 |
| Trailing 12-mo revenue > $2,000,000 | Reviewed annually; not to exceed $180,000 without 75% approval |

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) **Automatic adjustments:** Base Salary steps up automatically on the first day of the calendar quarter following the quarter in which trailing-twelve-month Company revenue crosses a threshold. Step-downs require 75% approval.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) **Annual CPI adjustment:** Once the Managing Member is at or above the $90,000 tier, Base Salary adjusts annually on January 1 by the prior calendar year's CPI-U (all urban consumers, U.S. city average, not seasonally adjusted), capped at 4% per year.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(e) **Reasonable compensation standard:** Both Members acknowledge the Base Salary schedule is intended to constitute reasonable compensation for services rendered under IRC § 162 and applicable self-employment tax rules. If the Company elects S-corporation treatment, the Members shall in good faith reassess whether the schedule satisfies the reasonable compensation standard for that tax regime.

**4.2 Bonus.** The Managing Member may receive a discretionary annual performance bonus not to exceed 25% of Base Salary, subject to 75% approval. Bonus, if paid, is treated as a guaranteed payment and reduces Net Profits before distribution.

**4.3 Expense Reimbursement.** The Company shall reimburse the Managing Member for reasonable, documented business expenses incurred on behalf of the Company under an accountable plan conforming to Treasury Regulation § 1.62-2.

**4.4 Benefits.** The Managing Member is entitled to participate in any health, retirement, or other benefit plans the Company adopts, on the same terms as other comparable employees or service providers.

**4.5 No Compensation to Non-Managing Member.** The Non-Managing Member receives no salary, guaranteed payment, or fee for status as a Member, but may be separately compensated for specific services rendered under a written agreement approved under Section 3.3(h).

## ARTICLE V — ALLOCATIONS AND DISTRIBUTIONS

**5.1 Allocation of Profits and Losses.** Net Profits and Net Losses for each fiscal year shall be allocated to the Members pro rata in proportion to their Percentage Interests, subject to regulatory allocations required under Treasury Regulation § 1.704-1(b) and § 1.704-2.

**5.2 Mandatory Tax Distributions.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) **Intent.** The Members recognize that the Company is a pass-through entity and that each Member may incur federal, state, and local income tax liability on allocated Company income regardless of whether cash is distributed. This Section 5.2 is intended to ensure each Member receives sufficient cash to fund such tax liability.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **Tax Distribution formula.** For each fiscal year in which the Company has Net Profits, the Company shall distribute to each Member, in cash, an amount equal to:

> **Tax Distribution = Member's Allocated Net Profit for the year × Assumed Tax Rate**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;where the "**Assumed Tax Rate**" is 45% (intended to approximate the highest combined federal, Ohio state, and municipal income tax rate applicable to an individual resident in Ohio, inclusive of self-employment tax and net investment income tax). The Assumed Tax Rate may be adjusted by 75% approval to reflect material changes in applicable tax law.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) **Timing.** Tax Distributions shall be made in quarterly installments no later than the 10th day of April, June, September, and January (aligned with federal estimated-tax due dates), based on the Managing Member's good-faith estimate of each Member's allocated Net Profit for the relevant period. A true-up distribution shall be made within 30 days after the Company's tax return is filed for the fiscal year.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) **Priority.** Tax Distributions are **mandatory** and take priority over all other distributions (other than Base Salary and other guaranteed payments). Tax Distributions are treated as advances against, and reduce, future distributions made under Section 5.3.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(e) **Liquidity shortfall.** If the Company lacks sufficient cash to fund Tax Distributions in full, the Managing Member shall (i) first defer or reduce any non-mandatory Company expenditures, and (ii) if a shortfall remains, arrange a Company-level line of credit or short-term loan sufficient to fund the Tax Distributions. The Company shall not skip a Tax Distribution without the written consent of both Members.

**5.3 Discretionary Distributions.** After payment of Base Salary, guaranteed payments, mandatory Tax Distributions, reasonable working-capital reserves, and debt service, remaining cash may be distributed to the Members pro rata in proportion to their Percentage Interests, upon approval under Section 3.3(i).

**5.4 No Distribution If Prohibited.** No distribution shall be made if, after giving effect to it, the Company would be unable to pay its debts as they become due or if total assets would be less than total liabilities, consistent with ORC § 1706.30.

## ARTICLE VII — TRANSFER RESTRICTIONS

**7.1 General Restriction.** No Member may sell, assign, pledge, encumber, or otherwise transfer any Membership Interest except as permitted by this Article VII. Any attempted transfer in violation of this Article is void.

**7.2 Permitted Transfers.** A Member may transfer Units to (a) a revocable trust for estate-planning purposes where the Member retains full voting control, or (b) a wholly-owned single-member LLC disregarded for tax purposes, in each case with 30 days' prior written notice to the other Member and subject to the transferee agreeing in writing to be bound by this Agreement.

**7.3 Right of First Refusal.** If a Member ("Selling Member") receives a bona fide written offer from an unaffiliated third party to purchase any Units, the Selling Member shall deliver the offer to the other Member ("Non-Selling Member"), who shall have 45 days to elect to purchase the offered Units on the same terms. If declined, the Company shall have an additional 30 days to purchase on the same terms. If both decline, the Selling Member may close with the third party on terms no more favorable to the buyer than those offered to the Non-Selling Member, within 90 days.

**7.4 Tag-Along.** If the Managing Member proposes to sell more than 25% of the Units to a third party, the Non-Managing Member may elect to sell a pro rata portion of their Units on the same terms.

**7.5 Drag-Along.** If Members holding at least 75% of the Units approve a sale of the Company under Section 3.3(a), the remaining Member shall, upon request, sell their Units on the same terms and execute customary transaction documents.

## ARTICLE VIII — BUYOUT EVENTS

**8.1 Triggering Events.** A Member's Units shall be purchased by the Company (or, at the Company's election, by the other Member) upon:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) **Death** of a Member;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **Disability** of a Member, meaning the inability to perform material duties for 180 consecutive days or 270 days in any 12-month period, as certified by a licensed physician;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) **Voluntary withdrawal** upon 90 days' written notice (subject to Section 8.3);
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) **Bankruptcy** of a Member;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(e) **Divorce** resulting in a court-ordered transfer of Units to a non-Member spouse; or
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(f) **For Cause** removal under Section 9.3 (which is governed by Section 9.4 pricing).

**8.2 Purchase Price (Standard).** For Triggering Events 8.1(a)–(e), the purchase price is the departing Member's Percentage Interest multiplied by the Fair Market Value of the Company as determined under Section 8.5.

**8.3 Voluntary Withdrawal — Lockup and Discount.** A Member may not voluntarily withdraw under Section 8.1(c) within 3 years of the effective date of this Agreement. Voluntary withdrawal between year 3 and year 5 is priced at 85% of Fair Market Value. Voluntary withdrawal after year 5 is priced at 100% of Fair Market Value.

**8.4 Payment Terms.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) **Down payment:** 20% of the purchase price at closing.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **Promissory note:** The balance in a promissory note from the purchaser to the departing Member (or estate), payable in equal monthly installments over 5 years, bearing simple interest at the Applicable Federal Rate (mid-term) in effect on the closing date plus 2%, secured by a pledge of the purchased Units.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) **Life insurance funding:** The Company may (and is encouraged to) purchase cross-purchase or entity-redemption life insurance on each Member in an amount approximating their estimated interest; insurance proceeds shall be applied first to the down payment and then to prepay the note.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) **Default:** If the purchaser defaults on the note and fails to cure within 60 days of written notice, the unpaid balance accelerates and the departing Member (or estate) may pursue any remedy at law, including foreclosure on the pledged Units.

**8.5 Fair Market Value Determination.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) **Annual agreed value.** On or before March 31 of each year, the Members shall in good faith agree in writing on a Fair Market Value of the Company ("Agreed Value") to be used for the following 12 months. If the Members agree, the Agreed Value governs.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **Appraisal fallback.** If the Members fail to agree within 60 days after a Triggering Event, each Member shall select an independent appraiser credentialed by the American Society of Appraisers (ASA) or holding the ABV (Accredited in Business Valuation) designation. The two appraisers shall each deliver a written valuation within 60 days. If the two valuations are within 10% of each other, the Fair Market Value is the average. If they differ by more than 10%, the two appraisers shall jointly select a third appraiser whose determination, constrained to a value between the first two, shall be binding. Appraisal costs are split pro rata by Percentage Interest.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) **Valuation standard.** Fair Market Value means the price a willing, unrelated buyer would pay a willing seller, neither under compulsion, in an arm's-length transaction, determined on a going-concern basis using customary valuation methodologies (discounted cash flow, comparable transactions, and asset-based approaches, weighted appropriately). Minority and marketability discounts shall **not** be applied for purposes of this Section 8.5 unless the Triggering Event is a For Cause removal under Section 9.4.

## ARTICLE IX — BAD ACTS, FOR CAUSE REMOVAL, AND MUTUAL PROTECTIONS

**9.1 Definition of Cause.** "Cause" means any of the following acts or omissions by a Member (the "Offending Member"):

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) Fraud, embezzlement, misappropriation of Company assets, or material dishonesty in dealings with the Company or the other Member;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) Conviction of, or a plea of no contest to, a felony or any crime involving moral turpitude;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) Willful misconduct or gross negligence in the performance of duties to the Company that causes material harm to the Company;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) Material and uncured breach of this Agreement, including breach of the non-compete (Section 9.6), non-solicit (Section 9.7), or confidentiality (Section 9.8) covenants, which remains uncured 30 days after written notice from the non-offending Member specifying the breach;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(e) Material violation of any final, non-appealable order of a court or regulatory authority arising from conduct on behalf of the Company;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(f) Misuse of Company funds for personal purposes exceeding $2,500 without reimbursement within 30 days of written demand; or
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(g) Conduct involving sexual harassment, abuse, or misconduct (particularly material given the Company's involvement with youth sports) that is substantiated by a final judgment, admission, or a credible independent investigation.

**9.2 Notice and Hearing.** The non-offending Member shall deliver written notice to the Offending Member specifying the facts alleged to constitute Cause. The Offending Member has 30 days to respond in writing. If the parties cannot resolve the dispute, either may invoke dispute resolution under Section 11.7; the arbitrator shall make a binding determination of whether Cause exists.

**9.3 Consequences of Cause Determination.** Upon a final determination of Cause:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) If the Offending Member is the Managing Member, they are automatically removed from the Managing Member role, and the Non-Managing Member becomes the Managing Member (or, by written agreement, a third-party manager may be appointed).
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) The Offending Member's Units are automatically offered for mandatory purchase under Section 9.4.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) The Offending Member forfeits any right to indemnification under Section 9.5 for the underlying acts.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) The Offending Member remains personally liable to the Company and the non-offending Member for damages caused by the Cause conduct; the Cause buyout does not release or waive such claims.

**9.4 Cause Buyout Price.** Upon a For Cause removal:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) The purchase price is **70% of Fair Market Value** (the "Cause Discount") multiplied by the Offending Member's Percentage Interest.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) Payment terms: 10% down at closing; balance over 7 years at the Applicable Federal Rate (mid-term) plus 2%, unsecured by the Units (to prevent the Offending Member from retaining any pledge leverage).
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) The Company may offset against the purchase price any damages, fines, restitution, or unreimbursed misappropriated amounts owed by the Offending Member to the Company.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) Between the Cause determination and closing, the Offending Member's voting, information, and distribution rights (other than Tax Distributions required by law) are suspended.

**9.5 Mutual Indemnification.**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) **Company indemnifies Members.** The Company shall indemnify each Member for expenses (including reasonable attorneys' fees), judgments, fines, and amounts paid in settlement actually and reasonably incurred in connection with any claim arising from the Member's good-faith actions on behalf of the Company, to the fullest extent permitted by ORC § 1706.31.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) **No indemnification for Cause conduct.** No indemnification is available for acts constituting Cause, fraud, willful misconduct, gross negligence, or acts from which the Member derived an improper personal benefit.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) **Cross-indemnification between Members.** Each Member (the "Indemnifying Member") shall indemnify and hold harmless the other Member (the "Protected Member") from any claim, loss, liability, or expense (including reasonable attorneys' fees) incurred by the Protected Member as a result of (i) the Indemnifying Member's Cause conduct, (ii) the Indemnifying Member's unauthorized acts purporting to bind the Company, (iii) the Indemnifying Member's breach of this Agreement, or (iv) the Indemnifying Member's personal obligations (e.g., personal tax liabilities, personal guarantees entered without Company authority, personal litigation).

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) **Insurance.** The Company shall maintain general liability insurance of not less than $2,000,000 per occurrence, sexual abuse and molestation (SAM) coverage of not less than $1,000,000 given youth sports exposure, employment practices liability insurance (EPLI), and cyber/data breach insurance appropriate to the Company's operations. Directors and officers (D&O) / management liability coverage shall be obtained once annual revenue exceeds $500,000.

**9.6 Non-Compete.** During the term each Member holds Units and for **2 years** after ceasing to hold Units, such Member shall not directly or indirectly own, manage, operate, consult with, or have a financial interest in any business engaged in youth or adult sports league operations, sports program registration software, or sports facility management within the State of Ohio or within 100 miles of any operating location of the Company, excluding passive investments of less than 2% in publicly traded companies.

**9.7 Non-Solicit.** During the term each Member holds Units and for **3 years** after ceasing to hold Units, such Member shall not directly or indirectly (a) solicit for employment or engagement any employee or contractor of the Company, (b) solicit as a customer any participant, family, coach, referee, sponsor, or venue partner of the Company with whom the Company transacted business in the preceding 24 months, or (c) induce any supplier, licensor, or franchisee of the Company to cease or reduce business with the Company.

**9.8 Confidentiality.** Each Member shall hold in strict confidence and not disclose or use for any purpose other than the benefit of the Company any Confidential Information (including software source code, customer lists, pricing, financial results, strategic plans, and trade secrets), during the term and indefinitely after ceasing to hold Units.

**9.9 Reasonableness and Blue Pencil.** Each Member acknowledges that the restrictions in Sections 9.6–9.8 are reasonable in scope and duration and necessary to protect the Company's legitimate business interests. If a court finds any restriction unreasonable, the court is authorized to reduce the scope or duration to the maximum permitted by Ohio law and enforce it as so modified.

**9.10 Injunctive Relief.** Each Member acknowledges that a breach of Sections 9.6–9.8 would cause irreparable harm for which monetary damages are inadequate, and that the non-breaching Member and the Company are entitled to injunctive relief without the requirement to post a bond, in addition to any other remedies.

## ARTICLE X — DISSOLUTION

**10.1 Events of Dissolution.** The Company shall dissolve upon:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(a) Written agreement of Members holding at least 75% of the Units;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(b) Sale of all or substantially all of the Company's assets and distribution of proceeds;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(c) Entry of a judicial decree of dissolution under ORC § 1706.47;
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(d) The Company having no Members for 90 consecutive days.

The death, Disability, bankruptcy, withdrawal, or For Cause removal of a Member shall **not** cause dissolution so long as a buyout under Article VIII or IX is consummated; the Company shall continue with the remaining Member.

**10.2 Winding Up.** Upon dissolution, the Managing Member (or if removed, a liquidator agreed by both Members, or absent agreement, appointed by the arbitrator under Section 11.7) shall wind up affairs, liquidate assets, and distribute proceeds in the following order: (a) to creditors, including Members who are creditors; (b) to Members in satisfaction of unpaid Tax Distributions; (c) to Members in proportion to their positive Capital Account balances.

**10.3 No Forced Sale of Software IP Below Fair Value.** In any dissolution or liquidation, the Company's core software assets and trademarks shall be offered for sale by competitive process (minimum 30-day marketing period, at least three solicited bids) to prevent a below-value sale to an insider.

# DRAFTING NOTES (DELETE BEFORE EXECUTION)

**Design choices to sanity-check with your attorney:**

1. **75% supermajority on major decisions.** A 51/49 split with simple-majority control means the 49% partner has no voting power. 75% supermajority on 9 categories of "Major Decisions" gives the minority partner real veto rights on dilution, sale, debt, amendments. This is the single biggest lever in the agreement.

2. **Base Salary schedule tied to revenue tiers.** Pre-agreed step-ups avoid the annual "what should I pay myself" negotiation. Year 1 at $60K, capped at $180K without supermajority. Reasonable compensation matters especially if you elect S-corp status later.

3. **45% assumed tax rate for Tax Distributions.** Ohio top marginal + federal top marginal + SE tax + NIIT approaches this. Conservative is better — ensures neither partner writes a personal check on April 15 because the LLC retained too much cash.

4. **Cause buyout at 70% of FMV, 10/90 payment split over 7 years, unsecured.** Punitive by design — the Cause Discount and unsecured note prevent the bad actor from holding the Company hostage. Some attorneys push for 50–60%; 70% is more enforceable middle ground.

5. **Non-compete: 2 years, Ohio + 100-mile radius.** Ohio enforces non-competes if reasonable, especially for owner-sellers. The blue-pencil clause (9.9) lets a court narrow rather than void if law shifts.

6. **Sexual misconduct as explicit Cause (9.1(g)).** Given youth sports exposure, this is a standalone trigger with a lower evidentiary threshold than criminal conviction. Do not weaken it.

7. **SAM insurance requirement (9.5(d)).** Sexual abuse & molestation coverage is mandatory for youth sports operators — cheap relative to exposure.

8. **3-year voluntary withdrawal lockup.** Prevents either partner from walking in the fragile first 3 years.

**What this draft does not cover — consider adding:**

- **IP assignment:** If you're contributing the existing Aspire Sports software as Capital Contribution, execute a separate IP Assignment Agreement alongside this OA and document the contribution in Section 2.1.
- **Key-person life insurance** specifics (mechanics depend on valuation).
- **Non-Managing Member information rights** beyond annual financials (monthly dashboards, board-style updates).
- **Deadlock-breaking mechanism** for the 9 supermajority items beyond dispute resolution (e.g., shotgun/Texas shootout, Dutch auction) — optional; 51/49 with supermajority vetos is already more resilient than 50/50.

**Before you sign:**

- Ohio business attorney review (mandatory).
- CPA review of tax provisions, especially Base Salary and S-corp election timing.
- Insurance broker quotes for SAM, EPLI, cyber, and D&O.
- **Independent legal counsel for the Non-Managing Member** — they should have their own lawyer, not yours. A single lawyer representing both is a conflict and weakens enforceability if ever challenged.
